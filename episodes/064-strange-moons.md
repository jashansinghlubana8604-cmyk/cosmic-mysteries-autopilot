## The Pasta Moons
mood: wonder
> i: pan moon saturn | v: saturn rings
Inside Saturn's rings, there is a narrow gap called the Encke Gap. And orbiting inside that gap is a tiny moon called {{Pan|PAN}}, about thirty kilometres across. It keeps the gap clear, sweeping up and pushing away ring particles as it goes.

> i: pan moon saturn | v: cassini spacecraft
In 2017, during its final months, Cassini flew close and photographed it. Pan looked exactly like a piece of ravioli. Or a walnut. A rounded body, with a sharp ridge running all the way around its equator, like a skirt.

> i: atlas moon | v: saturn moons
Scientists think the ridge is made of ring material that settled onto Pan's middle over billions of years, building up into a rim. Its neighbour, Atlas, has a similar shape, more like a flying saucer. [pause] Two moons that look like they came from a kitchen, not a planetary system.

> i: saturn f ring | v: saturn rings
Small moons like these are called shepherds, because they herd ring particles into place. Two of them, Prometheus and Pandora, guard Saturn's thin outer F ring. Prometheus dips into it on every orbit, pulling out streamers of ice like a finger dragged through sand. [pause] The ring is twisted, knotted and braided by moons you could walk around in a day.

## The Egg And The Dancers
mood: mystery
> i: methone | v: saturn moons
{{Methone|METHONE}} is even stranger. It is a tiny moon of Saturn, about three kilometres long. When Cassini photographed it in 2012, it was shaped like a perfect egg, and as smooth as one. No craters. No boulders. Nothing.

> i: methone | v: saturn moons
Almost every small body in the solar system is battered by impacts. Methone seems to be covered in an incredibly fine, fluffy layer of material, perhaps so loose that it flows like a liquid over billions of years, filling in any dents. [pause] Its density may be lower than that of water ice. A moon that could, in theory, float.

> i: janus epimetheus | v: saturn moons
Then there are {{Janus and Epimetheus|JANUS & EPIMETHEUS}}. Two small moons that share almost the same orbit around Saturn, separated by only about fifty kilometres. The inner one moves slightly faster, slowly catching up on the other.

> i: janus moon | v: saturn moons
They never collide. As the inner moon approaches, their gravity pulls on each other, and they swap. The inner moon is lifted into the outer orbit, and the outer one drops into the inner orbit. Then they drift apart again. [pause] Every four years, they trade places, in a dance they have performed for billions of years.

## The Wave Maker And The Wanderer
mood: wonder
> i: daphnis | v: saturn rings
In another gap in Saturn's rings orbits {{Daphnis|DAPHNIS}}, only about eight kilometres wide. Its orbit is slightly tilted, so it pulls the ring particles up and down as it passes.

> i: daphnis waves | v: saturn rings
The result is a series of waves in the edge of the rings, rising up to about one and a half kilometres high. Waves taller than the tallest skyscraper on Earth, made by a moon smaller than many cities, frozen in Cassini's images as delicate ripples of light and shadow.

> i: phoebe | v: saturn moons
Much further out lies {{Phoebe|PHOEBE}}. It orbits Saturn backwards, opposite to almost all the other moons, and its surface is dark as soot. It is probably a captured object from the outer solar system, a refugee from the Kuiper Belt.

> i: phoebe ring | v: saturn
And in 2009, the Spitzer Space Telescope discovered that Phoebe is shedding dust into an enormous, faint ring. The largest ring in the solar system, extending millions of kilometres from Saturn. If you could see it from Earth, it would appear about twice as wide as the full Moon. [pause] A ring so huge it was hidden in plain sight.

## The Wildest Orbit
mood: mystery
> i: nereid | v: neptune
At Neptune, there is a moon called {{Nereid|NEREID}}. Most moons follow nearly circular paths. Nereid's orbit is one of the most stretched of any moon in the solar system.

> i: neptune | v: neptune rotation
At its closest, it swoops to around one point four million kilometres from Neptune. At its furthest, it swings out to nearly ten million. Like a comet around its sun, but a moon around its planet. [pause] Scientists suspect Nereid's orbit was thrown into chaos when Neptune captured its giant moon, Triton.

> i: charon | v: pluto charon
And at Pluto, there is {{Charon|CHARON}}. It is so large compared with Pluto, about half its width, that the two do not really orbit one another. They both circle a point in empty space between them. Many astronomers think of them as a double world.

> i: charon red pole | v: charon
New Horizons found Charon's north pole stained a dark reddish brown. Scientists believe it comes from Pluto. Gas escaping from Pluto's thin atmosphere drifts across and freezes onto Charon's frigid pole during its long winter, where sunlight slowly turns it into reddish compounds. [pause] One world painting the other.

> i: charon canyon | v: charon
Charon is also split by a belt of enormous canyons, running for about a thousand eight hundred kilometres across its face, and in places several kilometres deep. Scientists think an ocean beneath Charon's crust once froze, expanded, and cracked the surface open. [pause] A moon that tore itself apart from the inside.

## A Moon For An Asteroid
mood: wonder
> i: ida dactyl | v: asteroid
And finally, the smallest member of our list. In 1993, the Galileo spacecraft, on its way to Jupiter, flew past an asteroid called Ida, about sixty kilometres long. When scientists examined the images, they found something next to it. A tiny moon, about a kilometre and a half across.

> i: ida dactyl | v: asteroid rotation
They named it {{Dactyl|DACTYL}}. It was the first moon ever confirmed around an asteroid. Before then, nobody knew whether something so small could hold onto a companion at all.

> i: asteroid | v: asteroid
Since then, astronomers have found hundreds of asteroids with moons, and even some with two. In 2023, NASA's Lucy mission flew past a small asteroid called Dinkinesh and discovered it had a moon too. And that moon turned out to be two pieces touching each other. [pause] Even the tiniest worlds can have families.
