## The Top Of The Clouds
mood: wonder
> i: jupiter | v: jupiter
First, the scale. Jupiter is more than three hundred times heavier than the Earth. It holds more than twice the mass of every other planet combined. You could line up eleven Earths across its face.

> i: jupiter | v: jupiter clouds
And it pulls hard. At the cloud tops, gravity is about two and a half times what you feel at home. If you weigh seventy kilograms here, you would feel like you weigh one hundred and seventy five there. Every step would feel like carrying another person on your back.

> v: jupiter clouds | i: jupiter clouds
You arrive at the cloud tops at more than sixty kilometres per second, pulled in by that enormous gravity. The air here is mostly hydrogen and helium, the same stuff the Sun is made of. The bands you see from Earth are clouds of ammonia ice, frozen at around minus one hundred and forty five degrees.

> v: jupiter storm | i: great red spot
Around you, the winds tear along at hundreds of kilometres an hour. Off in the distance, the {{Great Red Spot|GREAT RED SPOT}} turns, a storm wider than the Earth that has been raging for at least one hundred and fifty years, and probably far longer.

> i: jupiter lightning | v: jupiter lightning
As you drop through the ammonia, you pass into a layer of ammonium hydrosulfide, a cloud that smells of rotten eggs. Then water clouds. And here, flashes in the dark. Lightning, far more powerful than anything on Earth. [pause] And you are still only at the very top.

## The Last Probe
mood: dread
> i: galileo probe | v: galileo probe jupiter
Only one human object has ever fallen into Jupiter on purpose and reported back. In December 1995, the Galileo spacecraft released a small probe, about the size of a kitchen table, into the clouds.

> i: galileo probe | v: jupiter descent
It hit the atmosphere at nearly forty eight kilometres per second, the hardest entry ever survived by a human-made object. Its heat shield glowed hotter than the surface of the Sun. Then a parachute opened, and it began to sink.

> v: jupiter clouds | i: jupiter atmosphere
For {{fifty eight minutes|58 MINUTES}}, it sent back measurements. The winds got stronger the deeper it went, not weaker. The temperature climbed past one hundred and fifty degrees. The pressure reached twenty three times the air pressure on Earth.

> i: jupiter | v: jupiter storm
Then the signal stopped. The probe had gone about one hundred and fifty kilometres down. [pause] On Jupiter, that is barely a scratch. Like falling through the skin of an apple and never reaching the fruit.

## Where The Sky Becomes An Ocean
mood: dread
> v: jupiter storm | i: jupiter clouds
Keep falling. A few thousand kilometres down, there is no sunlight at all. The pressure is now thousands of times what you feel on Earth, and the temperature is thousands of degrees.

> i: jupiter interior | v: jupiter interior
And something strange happens to the hydrogen. It is squeezed so hard that it stops behaving like a gas, but it never becomes a normal liquid either. It becomes a hot, dense fluid with no surface at all. [pause] The sky simply thickens, and thickens, until you are swimming in it.

> i: jupiter juno | v: juno jupiter
There is no moment where the air ends and the ocean begins. No beach. No waves. Just a darkness that grows heavier with every kilometre.

> v: jupiter storm | i: jupiter interior
Of course, you would not be there to notice. Long before this point, the heat would have cooked you, and the pressure would have flattened anything with air inside it. Even a steel submarine, built for the deepest trench on Earth, would have been crushed thousands of kilometres above. [pause] Jupiter does not destroy you in one moment. It does it slowly, layer by layer.

> v: jupiter interior | i: jupiter interior
Roughly a fifth of the way to the centre, the pressure passes a million times that of Earth. And the hydrogen does something it almost never does. It turns into a metal. {{Metallic hydrogen|METALLIC HYDROGEN}}. Its electrons break free and flow, so it conducts electricity like copper.

> i: jupiter aurora | v: jupiter aurora
That ocean of liquid metal, churning as Jupiter spins once every ten hours, is a gigantic dynamo. It powers the strongest magnetic field of any planet, so vast that if you could see it from Earth, it would look bigger than the full Moon.

## Rain Inside A Planet
mood: mystery
> i: jupiter interior | v: jupiter interior
Down here, there is weather too. Scientists think that inside the metallic hydrogen, helium cannot stay mixed. It separates into droplets. And those droplets fall.

> v: rain | i: jupiter clouds
{{Helium rain|HELIUM RAIN}}. Falling through a sea of liquid metal, deep inside a planet, where no light has ever been. As it falls, it releases heat, one of the reasons Jupiter gives off more energy than it gets from the Sun.

> i: jupiter juno | v: juno spacecraft
Higher up, Juno found something even stranger. Hailstones, made of water and ammonia, growing into slushy balls called mushballs, plunging deep into the planet before they melt. Jupiter's weather reaches far deeper than anyone guessed.

> i: jupiter poles | v: jupiter poles
And at the north pole, Juno saw eight giant cyclones arranged in an almost perfect octagon around a ninth in the middle. Each one is about as wide as the United States. [pause] Nobody fully understands why they hold that shape.

## The Fuzzy Heart
mood: mystery
> v: juno spacecraft | i: juno spacecraft
For decades, the textbooks drew Jupiter the same way. Layers of gas and metal, and at the very centre, a small, hard ball of rock and ice. A seed, around which the whole giant had grown.

> i: jupiter interior | v: jupiter interior
Juno measured Jupiter's gravity more precisely than ever before, by watching how the planet tugged on the spacecraft during each pass. And the core was not small. It was not hard. It seemed to be smeared out, blending into the metallic hydrogen around it, perhaps reaching almost halfway to the surface.

> i: jupiter | v: jupiter rotation
Scientists started calling it {{a fuzzy core|FUZZY CORE}}. One idea is that, early in its life, Jupiter was struck by a young planet maybe ten times the mass of Earth. A head-on collision that shattered the original core and stirred it through the planet. Other scientists think the core simply formed that way. [pause] The debate is still open.

> v: jupiter interior | i: jupiter interior
At the very centre, if you could ever reach it, the temperature may be around twenty thousand degrees, hotter than the surface of the Sun. The pressure, tens of millions of times what you feel right now. [pause] And still, no floor. Just the deepest point of a planet that never truly decided to be solid.

> i: solar system planets | v: solar system formation
And this matters for us. Jupiter was almost certainly the first planet to form, and its gravity shaped where everything else ended up. Knowing how its heart was built tells us how our whole solar system was built, and how planets like it form around other stars. [pause] The answer to where Earth came from may be sitting at the bottom of Jupiter.

> i: jupiter juno | v: jupiter clouds
We look at Jupiter and see stripes and a red eye. But underneath, it is a world of crushing oceans, metal rain and a heart that refuses to stay in one place. [pause] The biggest planet in the solar system, and we are only now learning what it is made of.
