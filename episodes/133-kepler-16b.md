## The Discovery
mood: wonder
> i: kepler telescope | v: kepler
NASA's Kepler Space Telescope was designed to find planets by watching stars for tiny dips in brightness, the shadows of planets passing in front of them. Among its targets was a pair of stars in the constellation Cygnus, which eclipse each other as they orbit.

> i: binary star | v: binary star
The two stars of Kepler-16 circle each other every forty one days. One is an orange dwarf, about two thirds the mass of the Sun. The other is a small red dwarf, about a fifth of the Sun's mass. Seen from Earth, they regularly pass in front of each other, causing predictable dips in light.

> i: exoplanet transit | v: exoplanet transit
But a team led by astronomer {{Laurance Doyle|LAURANCE DOYLE}} noticed extra dips that did not fit the pattern. Something else was passing in front of the stars, sometimes the bigger one, sometimes the smaller one. The timing shifted from crossing to crossing, exactly as you would expect from a planet circling both stars. [pause] It was the first time a planet orbiting two suns had been confirmed so clearly.

> i: kepler telescope | v: kepler
The system turned out to be a gift for measurement. Because the stars eclipse each other and the planet crosses in front of both, astronomers could work out the sizes and masses of all three objects with unusual precision. The planet's gravity even slightly shifts the timing of the stars' own eclipses. [pause] A three-body dance, measured to remarkable accuracy from two hundred and forty five light-years away.

## Living Under Two Suns
mood: wonder
> i: kepler-16b | v: exoplanet
{{Kepler-16b|KEPLER-16 b}} is a gas giant, a bit smaller than Saturn, made mostly of gas and perhaps a large core of rock and ice. It circles both of its stars every two hundred and twenty nine days, at a distance a little smaller than Venus's orbit around our Sun.

> i: binary star sunset | v: sunset
But its stars are much dimmer than the Sun, so the planet is cold, perhaps around minus seventy to minus one hundred degrees at its cloud tops. It is not a desert like Tatooine. It is a frozen giant. [pause] But the view would still be extraordinary.

> i: binary star | v: binary star
From the planet, the two stars would appear close together in the sky, one orange and one red, slowly swinging around each other over weeks. Sometimes one would pass in front of the other, and the light would dim. And at the end of each day, on any moon of Kepler-16b, there would be a {{double sunset|DOUBLE SUNSET}}. Two shadows for every object. Two dusks.

> i: star wars | v: sunset
The discovery delighted the people who had imagined it. A visual effects supervisor from the company that made the Star Wars special effects joined the scientists at the announcement. [pause] Science fiction had predicted a world that turned out to be real.

## A Delicate Balance
mood: mystery
> i: binary star | v: binary star
Orbiting two stars is not easy. A planet too close to the pair would be pulled and pushed by each star in turn, its orbit growing chaotic until it was flung away or destroyed. Stable orbits only exist further out, typically more than a few times the distance between the two stars.

> i: circumbinary planet | v: exoplanet orbit
Astronomers call these worlds {{circumbinary planets|CIRCUMBINARY PLANETS}}, meaning planets around a binary. Many of the ones found so far orbit close to that inner edge of stability, as if planets tend to migrate inward until they reach the limit, and then stop.

> i: kepler-16b | v: exoplanet
The orbits also wobble. Because the planet is tugged by two stars, its orbit slowly shifts over time. That means it does not cross in front of its stars every orbit, as seen from Earth. Kepler-16b was expected to stop transiting for several years, before eventually lining up again. [pause] Even its shadow comes and goes.

> i: circumbinary planet | v: exoplanet
Kepler-16b is not the biggest. In 2016, astronomers announced Kepler-1647 b, a planet about the size of Jupiter, orbiting its two suns every three years, far out from the pair. At the time, it was the largest planet known to orbit two stars. [pause] Two-sun systems come in every size.

## The Family Grows
mood: wonder
> i: kepler-47 | v: exoplanet
After Kepler-16b, astronomers found more planets with two suns: Kepler-34b, Kepler-35b, Kepler-38b. Then {{Kepler-47|KEPLER-47}}, a system with at least three planets orbiting a pair of stars, one of them in the habitable zone, where water could be liquid if the planet had a surface.

> i: tess telescope | v: satellite orbit
In 2019, a seventeen-year-old high school student named Wolf Cukier was working as an intern at NASA. While examining data from the TESS planet-hunting satellite, he noticed a strange dip. It turned out to be a new planet orbiting two stars, now called TOI-1338 b. [pause] A teenager on his summer internship found a world with two suns.

> i: brown dwarf binary | v: binary star
And in 2025, astronomers reported something even stranger: strong evidence for a planet orbiting a pair of brown dwarfs on a path that passes over their poles, at right angles to the pair's own orbit. [pause] The universe keeps finding new ways to build planetary systems.

## Could Life See Two Suns?
mood: wonder
> i: binary star sunset | v: sunset
Could a habitable world orbit two stars? In principle, yes, if it sits in a stable orbit at the right distance. Its sunlight would vary slightly as the stars swung around each other, giving it unusual rhythms of light and warmth.

> i: exoplanet | v: exoplanet
Some astronomers have argued that, with so many stars in pairs, planets with two suns could be among the most common habitable worlds in the galaxy. If life exists on any of them, its creatures would grow up with two shadows and double sunsets as the most ordinary things in the world.

> i: alpha centauri | v: star field
Our nearest stellar neighbours make the point. Alpha Centauri A and B, two Sun-like stars, orbit each other just over four light-years away. In 2025, the James Webb Space Telescope reported evidence of a possible giant planet orbiting Alpha Centauri A, in its habitable zone, with the second sun shining brightly in its sky. [pause] If confirmed, our closest neighbour would be a planet with two suns.

> i: sunset | v: sunset
We live under a single sun, and imagine double sunsets as exotic. [pause] Somewhere out there, someone may be imagining a world with just one sun, and finding it lonely.
