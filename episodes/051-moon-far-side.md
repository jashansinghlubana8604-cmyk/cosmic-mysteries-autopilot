## Why We Only See One Face
mood: wonder
> i: earth moon | v: earth moon
The Moon spins on its axis once every twenty seven days or so. And it orbits the Earth in exactly the same time. So as it travels around us, it turns just enough to keep the same side pointed our way. This is called {{tidal locking|TIDAL LOCKING}}.

> i: moon | v: moon rotation
It did not start like this. Billions of years ago, the young Moon spun faster. But the Earth's gravity pulled on it, raising a slight bulge in its rock. Over time, that pull acted like a brake, slowing the Moon's spin until one side settled facing us for good.

> i: moon far side | v: moon
You may have heard the far side called the dark side of the Moon. It is not dark. It gets exactly as much sunlight as the near side. In fact, when we see a new Moon, the far side is in full daylight. [pause] It is only dark to us, because we have never been able to see it.

> i: moon | v: moon libration
Thanks to a slight wobble in the Moon's orbit, called libration, we can actually see about fifty nine percent of its surface from the Earth over time. But the remaining forty one percent stayed a complete mystery until the space age.

## The First Look
mood: mystery
> i: luna 3 | v: rocket launch
On the seventh of October 1959, a Soviet spacecraft called {{Luna 3|LUNA 3}} swung around behind the Moon and took the first photographs of its hidden side. It developed the film on board, scanned it, and radioed the grainy images back to the Earth.

> i: luna 3 far side | v: moon far side
They were blurry. But they were enough to surprise everyone. The near side is covered in wide, dark plains, the ones that make up the face of the Man in the Moon. The far side had almost none of them. Instead, it was crowded with craters, layer upon layer.

> i: apollo 8 | v: apollo 8 earthrise
Nine years later, in December 1968, the crew of Apollo 8 became the first humans to see the far side with their own eyes. As they passed behind the Moon, their radio link to the Earth was cut off. For the first time, human beings were completely out of contact with everyone else alive.

> i: apollo 8 earthrise | v: earthrise
And when they came back around, they saw something no one had ever seen. The Earth, rising over the lunar horizon. [pause] Their photograph, Earthrise, became one of the most famous images ever taken.

## Two Faces
mood: mystery
> i: moon near side | v: moon
The dark patches on the near side are called {{maria|MARIA}}, from the Latin for seas. Early astronomers thought they were oceans. They are actually huge plains of ancient lava, which welled up from inside the Moon billions of years ago and flooded giant impact basins.

> i: moon far side lro | v: moon far side
On the near side, maria cover about a third of the surface. On the far side, only a couple of percent. Its crust is also thicker, by many kilometres. Something made the two halves grow up differently.

> i: earth moon formation | v: moon formation
One idea is that, when the Moon was young, the Earth was still glowing hot from its own violent birth. The side facing the Earth stayed warmer for longer, while the far side cooled faster and built up a thicker crust. [pause] The Earth may have shaped the Moon's face simply by looking at it.

> i: moon impact | v: asteroid impact
Other scientists suspect a giant impact, or a second, smaller moon that once crashed into the far side and plastered it with extra rock. There is still no agreement. [pause] The answer is locked in the rocks of the far side itself.

## The Biggest Crater
mood: dread
> i: south pole aitken basin | v: moon far side
And the far side holds one of the largest impact craters in the entire solar system. The {{South Pole-Aitken basin|SOUTH POLE-AITKEN BASIN}} is about two and a half thousand kilometres across, wider than the whole of Western Europe, and up to around eight kilometres deep.

> i: asteroid impact | v: asteroid impact
Something enormous hit the Moon there, more than four billion years ago. The impact may have dug deep into the lunar crust, perhaps even reaching the mantle below. [pause] Which means rocks from deep inside the Moon might be lying on the surface, waiting to be picked up.

> i: chang'e 4 | v: lunar lander
In January 2019, China's {{Chang'e 4|CHANG'E 4}} made the first ever soft landing on the far side, inside this basin. Because there is no direct line of sight to the Earth, it relayed its signals through a satellite parked far behind the Moon. Its little rover, Yutu-2, kept driving for years.

> i: yutu 2 rover | v: lunar rover
Chang'e 4 also carried a small sealed container of seeds and soil. A cotton seed inside it sprouted, the first plant ever to grow on the Moon, before freezing in the long lunar night. [pause] For a few days, there was life on the far side.

## Pieces Of The Hidden World
mood: wonder
> i: chang'e 6 | v: lunar lander
In June 2024, China's {{Chang'e 6|CHANG'E 6}} landed inside the South Pole-Aitken basin, drilled and scooped up rock and soil, launched it back into lunar orbit, and sent it home. On the twenty fifth of June, a capsule landed in Inner Mongolia carrying almost two kilograms of the far side.

> i: lunar sample | v: lunar sample
For the first time in history, humans could hold pieces of the Moon's hidden face. Early studies found volcanic rock that erupted on the far side around two point eight billion years ago, and hints of much older volcanism too. The far side, it turns out, was not as quiet as it looked.

> i: moon far side | v: moon far side
Scientists have also started comparing the far side's interior with the near side's. Some results suggest the mantle beneath the far side is drier, with less water locked into its rocks. Piece by piece, the two faces of the Moon are starting to make sense.

> i: moon | v: moon rotation
The far side may also become one of the most valuable places for science. Shielded from all the radio noise of the Earth, it is the quietest place in our neighbourhood. Astronomers dream of building radio telescopes there, to listen to the faintest whispers of the early universe.

> i: earthrise | v: earth moon
So tonight, when you look up at the Moon, remember. You are seeing only half of it. Behind that familiar face lies a scarred, ancient, hidden world. [pause] And for the first time, we are starting to understand why it turned its back on us.
