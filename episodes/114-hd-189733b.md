## One Of The Most Studied Worlds
mood: wonder
> i: dumbbell nebula | v: night sky
The planet orbits a star called HD 189733, in the small constellation Vulpecula, the little fox, close in the sky to the beautiful Dumbbell Nebula. The star is a little smaller and cooler than the Sun, and can be seen with binoculars. It even has a faint red dwarf companion star, orbiting far out, so the planet's sky would hold a second, dimmer sun.

> i: exoplanet transit | v: exoplanet transit
The planet, {{HD 189733 b|HD 189733 b}}, was discovered in 2005, as it passed in front of its star, dimming its light by about two and a half percent every two point two days. That large, frequent dip, around a bright, nearby star, made it a dream target.

> i: telescope dome | v: telescope night
In 2008, astronomers using a telescope on the ground detected sodium in its atmosphere, one of the first times the air of an alien world had been measured from the Earth's surface rather than from space. [pause] Every new technique for studying exoplanet atmospheres seemed to be tested on this one planet first.

> i: spitzer telescope | v: spitzer
It became one of the most studied planets outside our solar system. In 2007, astronomers used the Spitzer Space Telescope to watch it over a full orbit, measuring how its brightness changed as different parts of the planet turned towards us. [pause] From that, they built the first ever temperature map of a planet around another star.

> i: hd 189733b artist impression | v: exoplanet
The map showed that the hottest spot was not directly under the star, but shifted to one side. The planet's fierce winds were carrying heat away from the point facing its star. [pause] For the first time, we were seeing the weather on an alien world.

## The True Blue
mood: wonder
> i: hubble space telescope | v: hubble space telescope
In 2013, astronomers used Hubble to measure the colour of the planet. They watched the system as the planet slipped behind its star and measured how much of each colour of light disappeared. The light that vanished was the light the planet had been reflecting.

> i: blue exoplanet | v: exoplanet
The result: HD 189733 b is {{deep blue|DEEP BLUE}}. It reflects blue light much more than red. It was the first time anyone had determined the actual colour of a planet around another star.

> i: hd 189733b | v: exoplanet atmosphere
On the Earth, blue comes from oceans reflecting the sky and from air scattering sunlight. On HD 189733 b, scientists think the colour comes from high clouds of tiny particles, perhaps made of silicates, the minerals that make up glass and sand, which scatter blue light. [pause] A blue world, coloured not by water but by grit.

> i: hd 189733b | v: exoplanet
And it is not bright. Overall, the planet reflects only a small fraction of the light that hits it. It absorbs most red light and much of the rest, returning a modest blue glow. [pause] A dark world with a blue tint, rather than a shining sapphire.

## Glass In The Wind
mood: dread
> i: hot jupiter | v: hot jupiter
HD 189733 b orbits about four and a half million kilometres from its star, around thirteen times closer than Mercury is to the Sun. It is almost certainly tidally locked, with one side always facing the star. The day side is scorching. The night side is cooler.

> i: storm | v: storm clouds
That temperature difference drives enormous winds. In 2015, astronomers measured the speed of the atmosphere directly, by tracking how the light from different sides of the planet was shifted. They found winds of about {{8,700 km/h|8,700 KM/H}}, blowing from the day side to the night side.

> i: hd 189733b artist impression | v: storm
If silicate particles condense in its clouds and fall as rain, they would be swept along by those winds. Not falling. Flying. A sideways storm of molten or solid glass, moving several times faster than the speed of sound. [pause] On this planet, the weather itself would be a weapon.

> i: hot jupiter | v: exoplanet atmosphere
The exact nature of the particles is still being studied. But every measurement makes it clear that HD 189733 b is one of the most violent atmospheres ever observed.

## The Smell Of A Planet
mood: mystery
> i: webb telescope | v: webb telescope
In 2024, a team led by astronomer Guangwei Fu used the James Webb Space Telescope to study the planet's atmosphere in infrared light. They detected water vapour, carbon dioxide and carbon monoxide. And they found something new.

> i: hd 189733b | v: exoplanet atmosphere
{{Hydrogen sulphide|HYDROGEN SULPHIDE}}. A molecule of two hydrogen atoms and one sulphur atom. The gas responsible for the smell of rotten eggs. It was the first time it had been detected on a planet beyond our solar system.

> i: exoplanet atmosphere | v: exoplanet atmosphere
That matters for more than its smell. Sulphur is an important ingredient in planet building. Measuring it helps scientists work out how planets like this one formed, and what materials they swept up. The team found that HD 189733 b has a mix of heavier elements broadly similar to Jupiter's.

> i: hd 189733b artist impression | v: exoplanet
The planet is also slowly losing its atmosphere. Its star is magnetically active and blasts it with radiation and flares. Observations have detected hydrogen escaping from the planet in a tail. [pause] It is being slowly evaporated by the star it orbits.

## Beauty And Terror
mood: wonder
> i: blue exoplanet | v: exoplanet
From far away, HD 189733 b looks like a jewel. Deep blue, glowing in the light of its star. Up close, it is one of the most hostile places imaginable. Searing heat. Winds faster than a bullet. Glass in the air. A sky that reeks.

> i: earth from space | v: earth from space
It is a reminder of something important. Colour alone tells us very little. The Earth is blue because of water and air. Another world can be blue for completely different reasons. [pause] When we eventually find a pale blue dot around another star, we will have to look very carefully before we call it a twin.

> i: webb telescope | v: webb telescope
Planets like HD 189733 b are where astronomers learn their craft. Every technique used to read its atmosphere, to map its temperature and measure its winds, will one day be turned towards smaller, cooler worlds. [pause] Studying the hellish blue giant is practice for finding a gentler blue world.

> i: hd 189733b | v: exoplanet
Sixty four light-years away, beneath a swollen orange sun, a blue giant spins in the dark, its winds howling at supersonic speed. [pause] It is beautiful. It is terrifying. And it is real.
