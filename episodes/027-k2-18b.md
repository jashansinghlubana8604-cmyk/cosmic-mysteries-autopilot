## Reading The Light Of A Distant World
mood: wonder
> v: exoplanet transit | i: exoplanet transit
We cannot photograph K2-18b directly. It is far too faint, lost in the glare of its star. But we can still study its air.

> i: exoplanet atmosphere | v: exoplanet atmosphere
When the planet passes in front of its star, a tiny fraction of the starlight shines through the thin edge of its atmosphere on the way to us. Different gases absorb different colours of light. By measuring which colours are missing, astronomers can work out what the atmosphere is made of.

> v: webb telescope | i: james webb space telescope
This method is extraordinarily delicate. The signal is a change of just a few parts in ten thousand. It became truly powerful with the James Webb Space Telescope, which can split the infrared light of distant stars with unprecedented precision.

> i: red dwarf | v: red dwarf star
K2-18b orbits a red dwarf star, smaller and cooler than the Sun. Because the star is dim, the planet's tight orbit places it in the habitable zone, the region where a planet could be the right temperature for liquid water.

> i: kepler space telescope | v: kepler telescope
The planet was discovered in 2015 by NASA's Kepler Space Telescope, during its second mission, called K2, which is where its name comes from. In 2019, the Hubble Space Telescope detected signs of water vapour in its atmosphere, making it the first known planet in a habitable zone with water in its air. That made it one of Webb's most important early targets.

## A New Kind Of World
mood: wonder
> v: ocean planet | i: ocean world
K2-18b is not an Earth. It is about two and a half times wider and around eight and a half times heavier. Planets like this, between the size of Earth and Neptune, are among the most common in the galaxy. Yet there are none in our own solar system, so we do not really know what they are like.

> i: exoplanet ocean | v: exoplanet ocean
In 2023, Webb found methane and carbon dioxide in its atmosphere, and very little ammonia. A team led by Nikku Madhusudhan at the University of Cambridge argued that this pattern fits a type of world they had proposed, a {{hycean world|HYCEAN WORLD}}: a planet with a hydrogen-rich atmosphere over a vast, warm ocean.

> v: ocean planet surface | i: ocean planet
If they are right, K2-18b could be covered by an ocean far deeper than any on Earth. Conditions in the upper layers of that ocean might be suitable for microbial life.

> i: sub-neptune | v: gas planet
But other scientists have proposed very different pictures. K2-18b could be a mini Neptune, with a thick gassy envelope and no surface ocean at all. Or its ocean could be too hot, or even made of magma deep below. The same data can be explained by several kinds of worlds.

> v: deep ocean | i: ocean depths
Imagine an ocean hundreds of kilometres deep, far deeper than the Mariana Trench, under a thick hazy sky glowing with the reddish light of a dim star. No continents, no beaches, no land at all. Just water, all the way down to a crushing layer of exotic high-pressure ice. If something lives there, it would have evolved in a world utterly unlike our own.

## The Gas Of Life?
mood: mystery
> v: ocean waves | i: ocean phytoplankton
Then came the claim that made headlines. On Earth, a gas called {{dimethyl sulphide|DIMETHYL SULPHIDE}}, or DMS, is produced almost entirely by life, mainly by tiny plankton in the oceans. It is part of the smell of the sea.

> i: exoplanet atmosphere spectrum | v: exoplanet atmosphere
In 2023, the Cambridge team reported a faint hint of DMS on K2-18b. In April 2025, using a different instrument on Webb, they reported a stronger signal of DMS, or a closely related molecule, at levels thousands of times higher than on Earth.

> v: k2-18b | i: k2-18b
They were careful to say it was not proof of life. But they suggested that if confirmed, the most plausible explanation might be a living ocean. [pause] For a moment, it felt like the biggest discovery in history might be close.

> i: red dwarf flare | v: red dwarf star
There is another complication. Red dwarf stars can be violent, especially when young, blasting their planets with flares and radiation. A planet orbiting so close to one may have had its atmosphere stripped, or its surface sterilised. Whether life could survive around such stars is one of the biggest open questions in the search for life beyond Earth.

## The Doubters
mood: mystery
> i: scientists data | v: data analysis
Within weeks, other teams reanalysed the same data. Several found that the signal largely disappeared when they used different statistical methods or different ways of modelling the atmosphere. Some concluded that there was no strong evidence for DMS at all.

> v: chemistry space | i: comet
Others pointed out that DMS may not be such a reliable sign of life. Traces of related molecules have been found on a comet, and in interstellar space, where no life exists. If chemistry alone can make it in some conditions, finding it would not be enough.

> i: exoplanet | v: exoplanet artist impression
This is how science is supposed to work. An extraordinary claim needs extraordinary evidence. For now, most astronomers consider the DMS detection unconfirmed. More observations are needed, and they are being made.

> i: scientists telescope | v: telescope observatory night
In science, a discovery this important needs a very high standard of proof, usually a confidence level called five sigma, which means less than about a one in a million chance that the signal is a fluke. The 2025 K2-18b signal fell well short of that. Reaching it could take many more hours of Webb observations, and even then, scientists will have to rule out every possible non-living explanation.

## Where Else To Look
mood: wonder
> v: trappist-1 | i: trappist-1
K2-18b is not the only target. Forty light years away, the TRAPPIST-1 system has seven rocky planets, several in the habitable zone. Webb has been studying them, searching for atmospheres. The inner planets appear to have little or none. The outer ones are still being investigated.

> i: proxima b | v: proxima centauri planet
The nearest star to the Sun, Proxima Centauri, also has a planet in its habitable zone. Future telescopes, including giant observatories on the ground and NASA's planned Habitable Worlds Observatory, are being designed to directly image Earth-like planets and analyse their air.

> v: earth from space | i: pale blue dot
One day, we may find a planet whose atmosphere contains oxygen, methane and water together, a combination that is very hard to explain without life. Perhaps around K2-18b. Perhaps somewhere we have not yet looked.

> i: exoplanet artist impression | v: exoplanet
Until then, K2-18b remains a tantalising question mark. [pause] A distant ocean world, or a hostile ball of gas, that may, or may not, be the first place we have ever detected the breath of alien life.
