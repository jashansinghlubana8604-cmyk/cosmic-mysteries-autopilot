In the constellation of Aquarius, about six hundred and fifty light-years away, something is staring back at us. A glowing ring of blue and red gas, with a pale star at its centre, like the iris and pupil of a giant eye.

> i: ngc 7293 | v: planetary nebula
On the internet it is often called the {{Eye of God|EYE OF GOD}}. Astronomers know it as the Helix Nebula. It is one of the closest objects of its kind to Earth, and one of the most photographed sights in the sky.

> i: planetary nebula | v: nebula zoom
But the Helix is not a place where stars are born. It is a place where a star died. The glowing gas is the outer body of a star like our Sun, cast off into space only about ten thousand years ago.

> i: white dwarf | v: white dwarf
And the tiny point at the centre is what remains of its heart. [pause] When we look into this eye, we are looking at the future of our own Sun.

## A Giant Eye
mood: wonder
> i: helix nebula | v: helix nebula
The Helix is enormous. Its brightest ring is around two and a half light-years across, more than half the distance from the Sun to the nearest star. Fainter gas stretches even further. [pause] In our sky, it covers an area about half the size of the full Moon.

> i: helix nebula | v: nebula zoom
Yet it is too faint to see with the naked eye. Its light is spread so thinly that only long exposures reveal its colours. In 2003, the Hubble Space Telescope combined dozens of images with pictures from a telescope in Chile to make one of the most famous portraits of it ever taken.

> i: helix nebula | v: nebula
The colours come from different elements. Oxygen glows blue-green near the centre, where the radiation is strongest. Further out, hydrogen and nitrogen glow red. [pause] Each colour is a chemical fingerprint of the star that made it.

> i: planetary nebula | v: planetary nebula
Objects like this are called {{planetary nebulae|PLANETARY NEBULAE}}, a confusing name. In the 1700s, through small telescopes, they looked like the round discs of planets. The name stuck, even though they have nothing to do with planets.

> i: southern ring nebula | v: planetary nebula
Astronomers know of about three thousand planetary nebulae in our galaxy, and no two look alike. Some are rings, some hourglasses, some tangled shells. In 2022, the James Webb Space Telescope photographed one called the Southern Ring and revealed that its central star has a companion, whose orbit stirs the gas into layered patterns. [pause] Many of the strangest shapes may be sculpted by hidden partner stars.

## How A Star Dies Gently
mood: mystery
> i: red giant star | v: red giant star
The star that made the Helix was probably a little more massive than our Sun. For billions of years it shone steadily, fusing hydrogen into helium in its core. Then the hydrogen ran low.

> i: red giant | v: red giant star
Its core shrank and heated up, while its outer layers swelled to hundreds of times their original size. The star became a {{red giant|RED GIANT}}. Its surface grew so large and so weakly held that it began to drift away into space, carried off by powerful stellar winds.

> i: planetary nebula | v: star shedding layers
Over tens of thousands of years, the star shed most of its mass. Eventually, only its hot, exposed core was left. Ultraviolet light from that core then lit up the gas it had cast off, making it glow. [pause] That glowing shell is the nebula we see.

> i: white dwarf | v: white dwarf
The core is now a {{white dwarf|WHITE DWARF}}, an object about the size of Earth but with more than half the mass of the Sun. Its surface is extremely hot, around a hundred thousand degrees. A teaspoon of its material would weigh tonnes. [pause] It no longer makes energy. It will simply cool for billions of years.

> i: helix nebula | v: nebula
The glow is temporary. The gas is expanding outwards at tens of kilometres per second. Within a few tens of thousands of years, it will spread so thin and the white dwarf will fade so much that the nebula will vanish. [pause] On the scale of the universe, the Eye is a blink. Its star lived for billions of years, and this glowing farewell will last less than one percent of one percent of that time.

## Thousands Of Comets
mood: mystery
> i: helix nebula knots | v: helix nebula
When Hubble zoomed into the inner edge of the ring, it found something unexpected. Thousands of tiny objects, each with a bright head and a long tail pointing away from the central star. Because they look like comets, astronomers called them {{cometary knots|COMETARY KNOTS}}.

> i: helix nebula knots | v: nebula zoom
They are not real comets. Each is a dense clump of gas and dust, and each head is roughly the size of our solar system. The tails form where the clump shields gas behind it from the star's fierce light and wind. [pause] There may be twenty thousand or more of them in the Helix.

> i: helix nebula infrared | v: nebula
Their origin is still debated. They may form where the fast hot wind from the dying star crashes into slower, colder gas released earlier, breaking it into fragments. Inside them, astronomers have found molecules such as hydrogen gas, which should be destroyed by the harsh radiation. [pause] Somehow, these knots protect fragile chemistry in the middle of the storm.

> i: spitzer helix | v: dust disk
In 2007, the Spitzer Space Telescope found another surprise. Its infrared camera detected a disc of dust around the white dwarf at the centre. One explanation is that comets and other small bodies, surviving from the star's original planetary system, are still crashing into each other there. [pause] The dead star may still be surrounded by the wreckage of its worlds.

## Our Sun's Future
mood: epic
> i: sun | v: sun surface
In about five billion years, our Sun will follow the same path. As its hydrogen runs low, it will swell into a red giant, becoming hundreds of times wider than it is now.

> i: red giant sun earth | v: red giant star
Mercury and Venus will almost certainly be swallowed. Whether the Earth is engulfed is less certain, because the Sun will also lose mass and its grip on the planets will loosen. [pause] Either way, long before that, the Earth's oceans will have boiled away.

> i: planetary nebula | v: planetary nebula
Then the Sun will gently release its outer layers. For a few thousand years, it may glow as a planetary nebula, perhaps a faint ring like the Helix, visible to any astronomers in other star systems.

> i: white dwarf | v: white dwarf
What remains will be a white dwarf, roughly the size of the Earth, slowly cooling in the dark. [pause] The material it scatters, rich in carbon and other elements made in its core, will drift into space and one day join new clouds that form new stars and planets.

> i: helix nebula | v: helix nebula
That is the real meaning of the Eye in space. It is not watching us. It is a mirror, showing us how stars like ours end, and how their remains seed the next generations. [pause] The carbon in your body was once inside a dying star, cast off in a glowing cloud much like this one.

> i: helix nebula | v: nebula zoom
In about ten thousand years, the Helix will have faded from view. But right now, in this short window of cosmic time, we get to look into the eye of a dying star. [pause] And see, in its fading light, a glimpse of our own distant future.
