## How To Find A Hidden Ocean
mood: mystery
> i: galileo spacecraft | v: galileo spacecraft
You cannot see an ocean under kilometres of ice. So how do we know they are there? Scientists use clever tricks. One is magnetism. Salty water conducts electricity, and when a moon with a buried salty ocean moves through its planet's magnetic field, it creates a magnetic signature of its own.

> i: cassini spacecraft | v: cassini saturn
Another is gravity. By tracking a spacecraft's radio signal as it flies past, scientists can measure tiny differences in a moon's pull, revealing how its mass is arranged inside. And by watching how a moon wobbles as it orbits, they can tell whether its icy shell is floating free on liquid.

> i: enceladus plumes | v: enceladus plumes
And sometimes, the ocean comes to us. Plumes of water shooting out of cracks in the ice can be flown through and sampled. [pause] Using these methods, scientists have built a list of ocean worlds. Some are certain. Some are hopeful guesses.

> i: ice moon | v: ice moon
How can water stay liquid so far from the Sun? Three things help. Tides from a giant planet flex a moon's interior and warm it, like bending a paperclip back and forth. Radioactive elements in the rock slowly release heat. And salt or ammonia in the water act as antifreeze, keeping it liquid at temperatures well below zero. [pause] The ice on top does the rest, like a thick blanket.

## The Distant Maybes
mood: mystery
> i: pluto heart | v: pluto
Start at the edge of the solar system. On {{Pluto|PLUTO}}, New Horizons found a vast plain of frozen nitrogen shaped like a heart. Its position on the planet, and the way it seems to have tipped Pluto over, suggest there may be a dense layer of liquid water beneath it. A hidden ocean, almost six billion kilometres from the Sun.

> i: triton | v: triton
Neptune's moon {{Triton|TRITON}}, with its nitrogen geysers, may also hold an ocean beneath its frozen crust, kept liquid by heat from the time it was captured and squeezed by Neptune.

> i: titania | v: uranus moons
Around Uranus, the largest moons, Titania and Oberon, could have thin layers of salty water deep inside. And in the asteroid belt, the dwarf planet {{Ceres|CERES}} has bright patches of salt on its surface, left behind when brine seeped up from below. [pause] It may still have pockets of salty water underground today.

> i: ocean world moon | v: ice moon
None of these are confirmed. But every one of them has evidence that would have been unthinkable fifty years ago. [pause] Liquid water, in the cold, dark outer solar system.

## The Silent Giants
mood: wonder
> i: callisto | v: callisto
Moving inward, we reach {{Callisto|CALLISTO}}, the most cratered moon of Jupiter. Its surface looks dead and ancient, unchanged for billions of years. But when the Galileo spacecraft flew past, it detected a magnetic signal suggesting a salty ocean, perhaps a couple of hundred kilometres beneath the surface.

> i: titan | v: titan
And Saturn's giant moon, {{Titan|TITAN}}. Famous for its seas of liquid methane on the surface. But beneath its icy crust, Cassini found evidence of a second, deeper ocean of water, probably salty, wrapping the whole moon.

> i: titan surface | v: titan
Some estimates suggest Titan's hidden ocean may hold many times the water of all the Earth's oceans. A world with two kinds of sea. Methane lakes under an orange sky, and a vast, dark ocean of water far below. [pause] Titan may be the strangest ocean world of all.

> i: mimas | v: saturn moons
Even the smallest moons can surprise us. In 2024, scientists studying the way Saturn's little moon Mimas wobbles concluded that it, too, probably hides an ocean. A young one, perhaps only a few million to tens of millions of years old, beneath a battered surface that looks completely frozen and dead. [pause] If Mimas has an ocean, we may be underestimating how common they are.

## The Big Three
mood: wonder
> i: ganymede | v: ganymede
Then there is {{Ganymede|GANYMEDE}}, the biggest moon in the solar system. Hubble watched its auroras rock back and forth, and the way they moved revealed a deep salty ocean, maybe a hundred kilometres thick, beneath some one hundred and fifty kilometres of ice.

> i: europa | v: europa
{{Europa|EUROPA}}, smaller than our Moon, is covered by a cracked shell of ice. Beneath it lies an ocean perhaps around a hundred kilometres deep, which could hold roughly twice the water of all the Earth's oceans. And unlike Ganymede's, Europa's ocean probably sits directly on a rocky sea floor.

> i: europa surface | v: europa
That matters. Where water touches rock, chemistry happens. Minerals dissolve, energy is released, and the ingredients for life can mix. [pause] Europa may have had a warm, wet sea floor for four billion years. As long as life has existed on Earth.

> i: enceladus | v: enceladus
And finally, tiny {{Enceladus|ENCELADUS}}. Only about five hundred kilometres wide, but blasting jets of ocean water into space from cracks at its south pole. Cassini flew through them and found salt, organic molecules, hydrogen and phosphates. [pause] Every known ingredient for life, rising from a hidden sea and drifting into space.

## Why It Changes Everything
mood: wonder
> i: habitable zone | v: habitable zone
For decades, scientists talked about the habitable zone, the band around a star where a planet is the right temperature for liquid water on its surface. Ocean worlds broke that rule. These seas are far outside the habitable zone, kept warm by tides and internal heat instead of sunlight.

> i: jupiter moons | v: jupiter moons
That means life, if it can arise in water like this, might not need a planet like the Earth at all. It might only need ice, rock and a big planet nearby to squeeze it. And moons like that are probably common across the galaxy. [pause] The most common kind of habitable place in the universe might be a dark ocean under ice.

> i: europa clipper | v: europa clipper
We are going to find out. NASA's Europa Clipper is on its way to Europa, due to arrive in 2030. The European JUICE mission will reach Jupiter in 2031 and later orbit Ganymede. A drone called Dragonfly is due to arrive at Titan in the 2030s. And mission ideas for Enceladus are being studied.

> i: earth from space | v: earth rotation
We used to think we lived on the only ocean world. Now we know we live in a solar system full of them, hidden in plain sight, under ice we have looked at for centuries. [pause] Somewhere in that dark water, there may be something looking back up at the ice above it, with no idea that the stars exist.
