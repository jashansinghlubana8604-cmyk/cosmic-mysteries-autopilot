## The Ninth Planet
mood: wonder
> i: telescope observatory | v: observatory night
In 1930, a twenty four year old astronomer named Clyde Tombaugh was working at the Lowell Observatory in Arizona. His job was painstaking: compare pairs of photographs of the same patch of sky, taken days apart, looking for any dot that had moved.

> i: star field | v: star field slow
On the 18th of February, he found one. A faint speck, shifting slightly against the stars. It was a new world, beyond Neptune. It was named Pluto, a name suggested by an eleven year old girl in England, Venetia Burney.

> n: pluto charon | i: pluto charon
For decades, Pluto stayed mysterious. In 1978, astronomers discovered it had a large moon, Charon, so big compared to Pluto that the two orbit a point in space between them, like a double planet.

> i: kuiper belt | v: kuiper belt
Then, in the 1990s, astronomers began finding more icy worlds out beyond Neptune, in a region now called the {{Kuiper Belt|KUIPER BELT}}. Some were almost as large as Pluto. In 2005, one called Eris was found that seemed even more massive.

> v: pluto | n: pluto
In 2006, the International Astronomical Union decided on a formal definition of a planet. Pluto did not meet it, because it has not cleared its orbit of other objects. It was reclassified as a dwarf planet. The decision is still argued about to this day.

> i: pluto orbit | v: solar system orbits
Pluto's orbit is strange too. It takes two hundred and forty eight years to go around the Sun once, so it has not completed a single orbit since it was discovered. Its path is tilted and stretched, and for twenty years of every orbit, it actually comes closer to the Sun than Neptune does. They never collide, because Pluto is locked in a rhythm with Neptune, going around twice for every three of Neptune's orbits.

## The Flyby
mood: wonder
> n: new horizons launch | v: rocket launch
In January 2006, just months before that vote, NASA launched the New Horizons spacecraft toward Pluto. It was one of the fastest spacecraft ever launched, passing the Moon's orbit in just nine hours.

> v: new horizons jupiter | n: new horizons jupiter
It swung past Jupiter for a gravity boost, then spent most of the next nine years asleep, to save power, waking up occasionally to check its systems. Its target was so far away that light from the Sun took more than four hours to reach it.

> n: new horizons spacecraft | v: new horizons spacecraft
On the 14th of July 2015, it reached Pluto. It could not slow down or go into orbit. It had one chance. It flew past, photographing and measuring everything it could, then waited until it was well beyond Pluto to send the data home. For almost a day, no one on Earth knew whether it had survived.

> n: pluto heart | i: pluto
Then the signal arrived. New Horizons was healthy. And over the following months, as the data slowly trickled back across billions of kilometres, the true face of Pluto emerged.

## The Heart Of Ice
mood: wonder
> n: pluto heart sputnik planitia | i: pluto heart
The most famous feature is a vast pale region shaped like a heart. Its western half is a basin called {{Sputnik Planitia|SPUTNIK PLANITIA}}, about a thousand kilometres across, filled with frozen nitrogen.

> n: sputnik planitia | i: pluto surface
Its surface has no craters at all, which means it is young, perhaps less than ten million years old. And it is covered in giant polygon shapes, tens of kilometres wide. They are the tops of slowly churning cells of nitrogen ice, warmed from below and rising, like a lava lamp moving in slow motion.

> n: pluto mountains | i: pluto mountains
Around it rise mountains of water ice, as high as the Rockies. On Pluto, water ice is as hard as rock. Nitrogen glaciers flow down from the highlands into the basin, just like rivers of ice on Earth.

> n: pluto atmosphere blue | i: pluto atmosphere
When New Horizons looked back at Pluto, silhouetted against the Sun, it saw something beautiful. A thin atmosphere, with more than twenty layers of haze, glowing blue. [pause] Pluto has {{blue skies|BLUE SKIES}}.

> n: pluto surface | i: pluto
All of this happens at around minus two hundred and thirty degrees Celsius, where sunlight is a thousand times dimmer than on Earth. At noon on Pluto, the light is about as bright as dusk here. Scientists had expected a world frozen solid for billions of years. Instead, they found one that is still changing, still flowing, still being reshaped today.

## The Hidden Ocean
mood: mystery
> n: pluto | i: pluto surface
Then came the biggest surprise. The position of Sputnik Planitia is suspicious. It sits almost exactly opposite to where Charon hangs in Pluto's sky, as if the basin is unusually heavy and has rolled Pluto around until it settled there.

> i: pluto interior | n: pluto
One of the best explanations is that beneath the nitrogen ice, there is a subsurface {{ocean of liquid water|A HIDDEN OCEAN}}, pushing up under the basin and making it heavier.

> n: pluto ice volcano | i: cryovolcano
There are also strange mountains that look like ice volcanoes, called cryovolcanoes, which may have erupted water and ice relatively recently. For a world so small and so far from the Sun, that kind of activity was completely unexpected. Somewhere, Pluto is still finding heat.

> n: charon | i: charon
Even Charon has a story. A huge reddish cap at its north pole seems to be made of material escaping from Pluto's atmosphere, drifting across space and settling on Charon, where sunlight slowly turns it red.

> n: pluto bladed terrain | i: pluto surface
In one region, New Horizons found towering blades of methane ice, some as tall as skyscrapers, lined up in rows across the landscape. Nothing quite like them was known anywhere else in the solar system. Pluto, it turned out, was one of the most varied worlds we have ever seen.

## Beyond Pluto
mood: wonder
> n: arrokoth | i: arrokoth
New Horizons did not stop at Pluto. On the first day of 2019, it flew past a much smaller object, over a billion kilometres further out, called {{Arrokoth|ARROKOTH}}.

> i: arrokoth | n: arrokoth new horizons
Arrokoth looked like a reddish snowman, two flattened lobes gently stuck together. It is one of the most primitive objects ever visited, almost unchanged since the birth of the solar system four and a half billion years ago. It showed that some of the first building blocks of planets came together slowly, softly, rather than in violent collisions.

> v: new horizons deep space | n: new horizons
New Horizons is still travelling outward, now more than sixty times further from the Sun than the Earth is. Like the Voyagers before it, it will one day leave the solar system entirely.

> n: pluto | i: pluto
Planet or not, Pluto taught us something profound. Even at the cold, dark edge of the solar system, worlds can be active, beautiful and full of surprises. [pause] We just had to go there to see them.
