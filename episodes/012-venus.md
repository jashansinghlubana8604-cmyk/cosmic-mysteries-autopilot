## Earth's Twin
mood: wonder
> v: venus earth comparison | i: venus earth
Venus is almost exactly the same size as the Earth, with nearly the same mass. It formed from the same materials, in the same part of the young solar system, at the same time. In the beginning, the two planets may have been remarkably similar.

> i: venus clouds | v: venus clouds
But Venus is wrapped in clouds so thick that no telescope can see through them in visible light. For most of history, its surface was a complete mystery, and that mystery invited dreams of oceans, swamps and life.

> v: radio telescope | i: radar venus
The first hard clue came in the 1950s and 60s, when astronomers measured radio waves coming from the planet. They suggested a surface far, far hotter than anyone expected. Then the spacecraft arrived, and confirmed the worst.

> i: mariner 2 | v: venus flyby
In 1962, NASA's Mariner 2 flew past Venus and became the first spacecraft ever to successfully visit another planet. Its instruments confirmed what the radio astronomers had feared. Beneath the clouds, Venus was not a garden. It was an oven.

## Into The Furnace
mood: dread
> v: venus atmosphere descent | i: venus atmosphere
The surface of Venus is about {{four hundred and sixty five degrees|465 °C}}. That is hotter than Mercury, even though Mercury is much closer to the Sun. It is hot enough to melt lead and zinc, and it barely changes between day and night, or from the equator to the poles.

> i: venus surface | v: venus surface
The atmosphere is mostly carbon dioxide, and it is crushing. The pressure at the surface is around {{ninety times|90x}} that of Earth's, the same as being nine hundred metres under the sea.

> v: venus clouds sulfuric acid | i: venus clouds
High above, the clouds are made of droplets of sulphuric acid. And they move astonishingly fast, racing around the entire planet in about four days, even though Venus itself turns so slowly that one day there lasts longer than its year.

> i: venus | v: venus rotation
And it spins backwards. On Venus, the Sun rises in the west and sets in the east. No one knows for certain why. A giant impact, or the slow pull of its own thick atmosphere, may have flipped its rotation long ago.

## The Photographs From Hell
mood: dread
> i: venera lander | v: venera lander
Between 1961 and 1984, the Soviet Union sent a series of spacecraft called Venera to Venus. Many failed. Some were crushed during descent, because no one yet understood how brutal the atmosphere really was.

> i: venera 13 surface | v: venus surface
But in 1975, Venera 9 and 10 sent back the first photographs ever taken from the surface of another planet. And in 1982, Venera 13 landed and sent back the first colour images: flat slabs of rock under a dim orange sky, the light filtered through the thick clouds above.

> v: venus lander | i: venera 13
Venera 13 was designed to survive for about thirty two minutes. It lasted {{one hundred and twenty seven minutes|127 MINUTES}} before the heat and pressure destroyed it. It even recorded the sound of the wind, the first sound ever recorded on another planet.

> i: venus surface radar | v: magellan venus
Later, NASA's Magellan spacecraft mapped almost the entire planet using radar, which can see through clouds. It revealed a world of vast lava plains, more than a thousand volcanoes, enormous mountains and strange pancake-shaped domes.

> v: venus surface | i: venus surface
Imagine standing there for a moment, if you somehow could. The sky would be a heavy, dim orange, like a permanently overcast evening. The air would be so dense that walking would feel like wading through water. Every surface would glow faintly with heat. There would be no shadows to speak of, no stars, and no view of the Sun, only a hazy brightness overhead.

> i: venus express | v: venus express
Europe's Venus Express spacecraft spent eight years in orbit, from 2006 to 2014. It watched a giant swirling vortex over the south pole, measured the escape of hydrogen and oxygen into space, and found hints of hot spots on the surface that might be fresh lava flows.

## A Living Planet?
mood: mystery
> v: venus volcano | i: venus volcano
For decades, scientists argued about whether those volcanoes were still active. In 2023, two researchers went back to old Magellan radar images from 1991 and noticed something. A volcanic vent had changed shape and grown in just eight months, apparently filled by fresh lava. [pause] Venus may be {{erupting today|STILL ERUPTING}}.

> i: venus clouds | v: venus clouds
Then there was the claim that made headlines around the world. In 2020, a team reported detecting phosphine gas in Venus's clouds. On Earth, phosphine is associated with living things. Could microbes be floating in the clouds, where the temperature is milder?

> v: venus atmosphere | i: venus atmosphere
The result was quickly challenged. Other teams could not confirm it, and argued that the signal might be weaker than claimed, or come from other gases. The debate has not been settled. But it reminded everyone how little we still know about our nearest planetary neighbour.

> i: venus clouds | v: venus atmosphere
Some scientists still argue that the clouds of Venus are the most Earth-like place in the solar system beyond our own planet. About fifty kilometres up, the temperature and pressure are not far from those on Earth's surface. If anything could live on Venus today, it would have to live there, floating forever in acid clouds, never touching the ground.

## What Went Wrong
mood: wonder
> v: venus earth comparison | i: venus earth
So how did Earth's twin become this? The answer is a process called a {{runaway greenhouse effect|RUNAWAY GREENHOUSE}}.

> i: venus atmosphere | v: venus clouds
Venus is about thirty percent closer to the Sun than we are. If it ever had oceans, the extra heat could have evaporated water into the air. Water vapour traps heat, which evaporated more water, which trapped more heat. Eventually, the oceans boiled away, and the Sun's light split the water apart, letting the hydrogen escape into space.

> v: sun | i: sun solar flare
Whether Venus ever truly had oceans is still debated. Some models suggest it may have had a mild climate for billions of years. Others suggest it was never cool enough for liquid water to form at all.

> i: venus spacecraft | v: venus mission
New missions are being planned to find out, including NASA's DAVINCI probe, designed to plunge through the atmosphere, and Europe's EnVision orbiter. They will search for signs of ancient oceans, active volcanoes, and the history of the planet that went so wrong.

> v: venus | i: venus
Venus is a warning, and a mystery. A world born like ours, at the same time, from the same ingredients, that followed a completely different path. [pause] Understanding why may help us understand how special, and how fragile, our own planet really is.
