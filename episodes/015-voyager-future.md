## The Last Words
mood: wonder
> v: rocket launch | i: titan centaur launch
Voyager 1 launched on the 5th of September 1977. It flew past Jupiter and Saturn, sent back some of the most famous images ever taken, and then headed out of the solar system, never to return.

> i: heliosphere | v: heliosphere animation
In 2012, it crossed the heliopause, the edge of the bubble of particles that flows from the Sun, and became the first human object in interstellar space. And in late 2026, it is expected to reach a distance of {{one light day|1 LIGHT DAY}} from Earth. A radio message sent to it today would take a full day to arrive.

> v: deep space network antenna | i: deep space network
Its power comes from the heat of decaying plutonium, which fades a little more every year. Engineers have switched off heaters and instruments one by one to keep it alive. Within the next few years, there will no longer be enough power to run even one instrument. Voyager 1 will go quiet.

> i: voyager spacecraft | v: voyager spacecraft
But a spacecraft does not need power to keep moving. In the emptiness of space, there is almost nothing to slow it down. Voyager will simply coast, in silence, for as long as the universe allows.

> i: golden record | v: golden record
The record was assembled in 1977 by a small team led by the astronomer Carl Sagan. It holds a hundred and fifteen images encoded in sound, from diagrams of DNA to a sunset and a family eating together. It holds ninety minutes of music, from Bach and Beethoven to Peruvian panpipes and Chuck Berry's Johnny B. Goode. And it holds the sounds of Earth: thunder, birdsong, footsteps, a kiss, a mother greeting her child.

## Three Hundred Years To The Cloud
mood: wonder
> v: oort cloud | i: oort cloud
Even after crossing into interstellar space, Voyager is still within the Sun's gravitational reach. Far ahead lies the Oort Cloud, a vast spherical swarm of icy bodies, thought to surround the solar system like a shell.

> i: comet | v: comet
Voyager will reach the inner edge of the Oort Cloud in around {{three hundred years|300 YEARS}}. It will need around thirty thousand years to pass through it. In all that time, it will probably never come close to a single icy body. Space is that empty.

> v: star field slow | i: star field
Then, in about forty thousand years, Voyager 1 will make its closest approach to another star, a small red dwarf called Gliese 445. Even then, it will pass at a distance of about one point six light years. The star will be just a slightly brighter point among millions.

> i: red dwarf | v: red dwarf star
Its twin, Voyager 2, will pass about one point seven light years from another red dwarf, Ross 248, around the same time. And in about three hundred thousand years, Voyager 2 will drift within about four light years of Sirius, the brightest star in our sky.

## Orbiting The Galaxy
mood: wonder
> v: milky way galaxy rotation | i: milky way artist impression
Voyager is not really leaving our galaxy. It is far too slow. Instead, like the Sun and every star nearby, it will orbit the centre of the Milky Way, completing one circuit around every two hundred and thirty million years.

> i: milky way centre | v: milky way centre zoom
Over billions of years, it will drift through the spiral arms, past stellar nurseries and dying stars, through clouds of gas and dust. The chance of it actually hitting a star is almost zero.

> v: dust in space | i: interstellar dust
Slowly, though, it will be worn away. Tiny grains of interstellar dust and cosmic rays will pit its surface, grain by grain. But the golden record was built for this. It was designed to last for {{a billion years|1,000,000,000 YEARS}} or more, protected by its aluminium cover.

> i: golden record | v: golden record cover
On that cover, etched instructions explain how to play it, and a map uses pulsars to point to the Sun. After a billion years, those pulsars will have slowed and faded, and the map will be out of date. But the message will still be readable to anyone who finds it.

> v: star field | i: dark space stars
Will anyone ever find it? Almost certainly not. Voyager is tiny, dark, and silent, and space is unimaginably vast. The record was never really a practical message. It was a gesture, a way of saying that we were here, and that we wanted to be known. As Sagan put it, launching it was a sign of hope.

## When The Sun Dies
mood: dread
> v: red giant sun | i: red giant
In about five billion years, our Sun will run out of hydrogen fuel in its core. It will swell into a red giant, perhaps large enough to swallow Mercury, Venus, and possibly the Earth.

> i: white dwarf | v: planetary nebula
Then it will shed its outer layers into a glowing shell of gas, and its core will shrink into a white dwarf, a dense, slowly cooling ember about the size of the Earth.

> v: andromeda milky way collision | i: andromeda milky way collision
Around the same time, the Milky Way may collide with the Andromeda galaxy. Recent studies suggest the collision is less certain than once thought, but if it happens, the two will merge into one giant galaxy over billions of years. Voyager, adrift among its stars, would be swept along with them.

> i: golden record | v: voyager spacecraft
By then, there may be no one left on Earth to remember the people who built Voyager. The record, carrying greetings in fifty five languages and the sound of a human heartbeat, may be one of the last traces that we were ever here.

## A Trillion Years
mood: wonder
> v: galaxy | i: galaxy
Go further still. Over hundreds of billions of years, the universe will keep expanding, faster and faster. Distant galaxies will slip beyond view. New stars will grow rarer, as the gas to make them runs out.

> i: red dwarf | v: red dwarf star
The last stars to shine will be red dwarfs, small and frugal, burning for trillions of years. In about a {{trillion years|1,000,000,000,000 YEARS}}, Voyager will still be out there, a cold metal frame orbiting among dimming red stars in a galaxy slowly going dark.

> v: black hole | i: black hole artist impression
Far beyond that, the last stars will fade. Black holes will slowly evaporate. The universe will become colder and emptier than we can imagine.

> i: voyager deep space | v: deep space flight
And somewhere in that darkness, perhaps, there will still be a small golden disc, carrying the music, the laughter and the voices of a planet that once existed. [pause] Voyager will not remember us. But for as long as it lasts, it will carry our story.
