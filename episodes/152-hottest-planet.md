> i: ultra hot jupiter | v: exoplanet
About six hundred and seventy light-years away, in the constellation Cygnus, there is a giant planet so close to its star that a year there lasts just a day and a half.

> i: kelt-9b | v: hot jupiter
On its day side, the temperature reaches around {{four thousand three hundred degrees Celsius|4,300°C}}. That is hotter than the surface of most stars in our galaxy. [pause] It is hotter than many stars that shine on their own.

> i: hot jupiter | v: exoplanet atmosphere
At those temperatures, molecules cannot survive. Water, methane, carbon dioxide, all are torn apart into single atoms. Metals like iron and titanium do not form clouds or rocks. [pause] They float in the air as vapour.

> i: blue star | v: star
And the planet is evaporating. Its star is blasting it with so much radiation that its atmosphere is streaming away into space. [pause] This is {{KELT-9b|KELT-9b}}, the hottest planet ever discovered.

## Found With A Camera Lens
mood: wonder
> i: small telescope | v: telescope observatory
KELT-9b was found by an unusual telescope. KELT stands for the {{Kilodegree Extremely Little Telescope|KELT}}, a joke name for a system built around ordinary camera lenses, not giant mirrors. Small robotic telescopes in Arizona and South Africa watched large patches of the sky night after night.

> i: exoplanet transit | v: planet transit
They were looking for stars that dim very slightly and regularly, the sign of a planet passing in front of them. In 2016, they spotted one dimming every day and a half around a bright star called KELT-9. [pause] The discovery was announced in 2017, and its temperature stunned astronomers.

> i: blue star | v: blue star
The star is the reason. KELT-9 is not like our Sun. It is about two and a half times as massive and burns far hotter, at around ten thousand degrees on its surface. It shines a brilliant blue-white and pours out intense ultraviolet light.

> i: hot jupiter | v: exoplanet orbit
And the planet hugs it tightly. KELT-9b orbits about thirty times closer to its star than the Earth is to the Sun. [pause] Its year lasts just thirty six hours.

## A Giant In The Furnace
mood: dread
> i: hot jupiter | v: hot jupiter
KELT-9b is a gas giant, nearly three times as massive as Jupiter and almost twice as wide. Its size is partly because of the heat. The searing radiation has puffed up its atmosphere like a balloon.

> i: tidally locked planet | v: exoplanet
Like many planets this close to their stars, it is probably {{tidally locked|TIDALLY LOCKED}}, with one side permanently facing the star and the other in permanent night. The day side is an endless glare of blue-white light filling much of the sky.

> i: red dwarf | v: star
At around four thousand three hundred degrees on the day side, it is hotter than red dwarf stars, the most common type of star in the galaxy, which often have surfaces of around three thousand degrees. [pause] If KELT-9b were floating alone in space at that temperature, you might mistake it for a small star.

> i: exoplanet orbit | v: exoplanet orbit
Its orbit is strange too. Most planets circle above their star's equator. KELT-9b travels on a path that carries it over the star's poles. [pause] Something in its past, perhaps a gravitational tug from another body, threw it onto that tilted orbit.

> i: blue star | v: star rotating
The star spins so fast that it bulges at its equator. That makes its poles hotter and brighter than its middle, an effect called gravity darkening. [pause] As KELT-9b crosses from pole to equator to pole, it passes over brighter and dimmer parts of the star, and astronomers can see that lopsided shadow in their measurements.

## Skies Of Iron
mood: mystery
> i: exoplanet atmosphere | v: exoplanet atmosphere
When a planet passes in front of its star, a little starlight filters through its atmosphere. Different atoms absorb different colours of light, leaving fingerprints that astronomers can read.

> i: iron | v: molten metal
In 2018, astronomers studying KELT-9b's light found clear signs of {{iron|IRON}} and {{titanium|TITANIUM}} atoms floating in its atmosphere. Not as dust or droplets, but as individual atoms, ripped apart from any molecules by the heat. [pause] Its sky is filled with vaporised metal.

> i: exoplanet atmosphere | v: exoplanet atmosphere
Later studies found more. Magnesium, sodium, calcium, chromium and other metals have all been reported in its atmosphere, many with electrons stripped away by the heat. [pause] It reads less like the air of a planet and more like the outer layers of a star.

> i: hot jupiter | v: exoplanet atmosphere
Even hydrogen molecules, the most common molecules in the universe, break apart on the day side. On the night side, the atoms may join back together. Some scientists think this cycle carries heat around the planet, like a kind of chemical weather.

> i: molten metal | v: lava
Other ultra-hot planets show similar extremes. On WASP-76b, astronomers think iron evaporates on the day side and may rain down as liquid iron on the night side. On WASP-121b, the James Webb telescope found winds that whip around the planet faster than any jet stream known. [pause] KELT-9b is hotter than all of them.

## A Planet Losing Its Skin
mood: dread
> i: evaporating planet | v: planet atmosphere escaping
The intense ultraviolet light from KELT-9 is so strong that it heats the upper atmosphere of the planet until it boils away into space. Astronomers have detected glowing hydrogen extending far beyond the planet, escaping like the tail of a comet.

> i: evaporating planet | v: exoplanet
KELT-9b is losing a huge amount of gas every second. Over hundreds of millions of years, that loss could strip away a large part of its atmosphere. [pause] Some planets close to their stars may lose their atmospheres entirely, leaving only bare, scorched cores.

> i: blue star | v: star
The star itself is changing too. Stars like KELT-9 live fast. In a few hundred million years, it will run low on hydrogen in its core and begin to swell. [pause] When it does, it will almost certainly engulf the planet entirely.

> i: hot jupiter | v: hot jupiter
So KELT-9b's future is already decided. Either it will be slowly peeled away by the light of its star, or it will be swallowed by that star as it grows. [pause] Either way, this world is on borrowed time.

## Why Find It?
mood: wonder
> i: exoplanet | v: exoplanet
Why study a planet no one could ever visit? Because extreme worlds test our ideas. KELT-9b sits right at the boundary between planets and stars, where the rules of planetary atmospheres break down.

> i: james webb telescope | v: james webb telescope
Hot giant planets are also the easiest exoplanets to study. They are big, bright, and their atmospheres are puffed up and full of strong signals. [pause] They are where astronomers learn the techniques that will one day be used to study smaller, cooler, Earth-like worlds.

> i: exoplanet atmosphere | v: exoplanet atmosphere
Learning to read the iron in KELT-9b's sky is practice for reading the oxygen or water in the sky of a distant Earth. [pause] Today's furnace planets are training us for the search for life.

> i: night sky | v: night sky timelapse
And the star is bright enough that, from a dark site on Earth, it can be seen with a good pair of binoculars. [pause] Point them towards Cygnus, and somewhere in the field of view, you are looking at the sun of a planet that is boiling away.

> i: kelt-9b | v: hot jupiter
Somewhere in Cygnus, a planet circles a blazing blue star every day and a half. Its skies are metal vapour, its day side glows hotter than many stars, and it is slowly being blown away. [pause] It is the hottest planet we know. And it reminds us that the word planet can mean things far stranger than the eight worlds of our own solar system.
