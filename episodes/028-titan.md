## The Moon With A Sky
mood: wonder
> v: saturn titan | i: saturn titan
Titan is bigger than the planet Mercury. It was discovered in 1655 by the Dutch astronomer Christiaan Huygens. For three centuries, it was just a dot beside Saturn.

> i: titan atmosphere | v: titan atmosphere
In 1980, the Voyager 1 spacecraft flew past it, hoping to see its surface. Instead, it saw an orange ball of haze, completely opaque. Titan is wrapped in a thick atmosphere, made mostly of nitrogen, like Earth's. At the surface, the pressure is about one and a half times what we feel at sea level.

> v: titan haze | i: titan haze
The orange colour comes from a smog of complex organic molecules, made when sunlight breaks apart methane high in the atmosphere. Those molecules drift down like a slow, endless snowfall of soot onto the surface below.

> i: titan | v: titan rotation
For a long time, no one knew what was hidden under that haze. Some scientists predicted a global ocean of liquid hydrocarbons. To find out, someone would have to go there.

## Landing On Titan
mood: wonder
> v: cassini huygens | i: cassini huygens
In 1997, the Cassini spacecraft launched toward Saturn, carrying a passenger: a small probe built by the European Space Agency, named after Titan's discoverer, {{Huygens|HUYGENS}}.

> i: huygens probe | v: huygens descent
On the 14th of January 2005, Huygens separated and fell into Titan's atmosphere. Its parachutes opened, and for two and a half hours it drifted down through the haze, photographing the landscape below.

> v: huygens descent images | i: titan surface huygens
The pictures showed something astonishing. Bright highlands cut by dark, branching channels, exactly like river networks on Earth, running down toward what looked like a dried lakebed.

> i: titan surface huygens | v: titan surface
Huygens landed with a soft thud, on ground the consistency of wet sand. Around it lay rounded pebbles of water ice, smoothed like stones in a riverbed. It was the most distant landing ever made, and it remains the only landing on a world in the outer solar system.

> v: huygens descent | i: huygens probe
Huygens almost did not work at all. Years before arrival, engineers discovered a design flaw in the radio link that would have garbled most of its data. Flying on Cassini, they changed the spacecraft's path so the signal would arrive cleanly. Even so, a software error on the day meant one of the two radio channels was never switched on, and half of the planned pictures were lost forever.

## Seas Of Methane
mood: wonder
> v: titan lakes radar | i: titan lakes
Cassini kept studying Titan for thirteen years, using radar to see through the haze. Near the north pole, it found them: hundreds of lakes, and three great {{seas|SEAS OF METHANE}}.

> i: kraken mare | v: titan sea
The largest, Kraken Mare, is bigger than the Caspian Sea on Earth. Another, Ligeia Mare, may be over a hundred and sixty metres deep in places. They are filled with liquid methane and ethane, natural gas, liquid at Titan's freezing temperatures.

> v: titan rain | i: titan clouds
Titan has a full weather cycle, just like Earth's water cycle, but with methane. Methane evaporates, forms clouds, and falls as rain, slowly, in large drops, drifting down through the thick air. Rivers carve valleys. Seasons, each lasting more than seven years, move the storms around.

> i: titan dunes | v: titan dunes
Near the equator are vast fields of dunes, hundreds of kilometres long, made not of sand but of grains of solid organic material. Titan is a world built from the ingredients of life, frozen in place.

> v: titan lake glint | i: titan sunglint
In 2009, Cassini caught a flash of sunlight reflecting off one of the northern lakes, like the glint of the Sun on a calm sea. It was the first time we had seen sunlight sparkle off liquid on another world. The lakes are astonishingly smooth, with waves usually no taller than a few millimetres.

> i: ligeia mare | v: titan sea
But not always. In Ligeia Mare, radar images showed a bright feature that appeared, vanished, and reappeared over several years. Scientists nicknamed it the magic island. It may be waves, floating solids, or bubbles of nitrogen rising from the depths. Titan's seas are alive with change.

> v: titan atmosphere | i: titan haze
There is also a puzzle in the sky. Sunlight should destroy all of Titan's atmospheric methane within a few tens of millions of years. Yet it is still there. Something must be replenishing it, perhaps methane seeping out from the interior, or from ice volcanoes we have not yet clearly seen.

## A Hidden Ocean
mood: mystery
> v: titan interior | i: titan interior
Then Cassini found something deeper. By measuring how Titan's shape flexes under Saturn's gravity, scientists concluded that beneath the icy crust lies a {{hidden ocean|A HIDDEN OCEAN}} of liquid water, probably salty, perhaps hundreds of kilometres down.

> i: titan | v: titan
That means Titan may have two very different environments for possible life. A deep water ocean, sealed beneath the ice, and strange surface lakes of liquid methane, where any life would have to be built from completely different chemistry.

> v: titan lakes | i: titan lakes
Could life exist in liquid methane? No one knows. Some scientists have imagined cell-like structures made from nitrogen-based molecules that could survive in it. It is speculation, but Titan is the only place in the solar system where we could test it.

## Flying Over Another World
mood: wonder
> i: dragonfly mission | v: dragonfly titan
NASA is now building a mission to go back. It is called {{Dragonfly|DRAGONFLY}}, and it is not a rover or a lander. It is a nuclear-powered drone, a car-sized quadcopter that will fly through Titan's thick air.

> v: dragonfly drone | i: dragonfly drone titan
On Titan, flying is surprisingly easy. The atmosphere is four times denser than Earth's and the gravity is weaker than on our Moon. With wings, a person could probably fly there by flapping their arms.

> i: titan surface | v: titan surface flyover
Dragonfly is planned to launch in 2028 and arrive in the mid 2030s. It will hop from place to place, travelling dozens of kilometres, studying organic chemistry on the surface, searching for the chemical steps that came before life.

> i: red giant sun | v: red giant
And Titan's best days may still be ahead. In around five billion years, when the Sun swells into a red giant, the inner planets will be scorched. But far out at Saturn, Titan may warm up enough for water to become liquid on its surface for hundreds of millions of years. Long after Earth is gone, Titan could become one of the most habitable places left in the solar system.

> v: titan saturn | i: titan
Titan is a frozen echo of the early Earth, a world where the raw ingredients of life have been cooking under an orange sky for billions of years. [pause] And soon, for the first time, we will fly through its clouds.
