## Too Small To See
mood: mystery
> i: m87 galaxy | v: galaxy zoom
The galaxy {{M87|M87}} is a giant ball of a trillion stars. Astronomers had known for decades that its centre held something extraordinary: a black hole billions of times the mass of the Sun, firing a jet of particles thousands of light-years into space.

> i: m87 black hole | v: black hole
But even this monster is incredibly small in our sky. From fifty five million light-years away, the glowing ring around it appears as small as an orange sitting on the surface of the Moon, seen from the Earth. [pause] No ordinary telescope could ever see something that tiny.

> i: telescope dome | v: telescope
The sharpness of a telescope depends on its size. The bigger the dish, the finer the detail it can see. To see a ring that small, using radio waves, you would need a dish about the size of the entire Earth. [pause] Nobody can build that. So the astronomers did the next best thing.

> i: radio telescope | v: radio telescope night
They also had to choose the right kind of light. Visible light from the centre of a galaxy is blocked by gas and dust. But radio waves at a wavelength of about one millimetre can pass through, carrying light from the very edge of the black hole straight to us. [pause] It was the one window in the spectrum where the shadow might show itself.

## A Planet-Sized Telescope
mood: wonder
> i: event horizon telescope | v: radio telescope
They linked radio telescopes all over the world into one instrument. The {{Event Horizon Telescope|EVENT HORIZON TELESCOPE}}. In April 2017, eight observatories, on mountains in Hawaii, Arizona, Mexico, Chile and Spain, and at the South Pole, all pointed at M87 at the same time.

> i: telescope dome | v: telescope night
That meant the weather had to cooperate on several continents at once. Water vapour in the air absorbs these radio waves, so every site needed dry, clear skies. Each night, the team checked forecasts around the world and decided whether to observe. [pause] Over about a week in April 2017, they got lucky enough times.

> i: alma telescope | v: radio telescope night
Each telescope recorded the incoming radio waves, along with ultra-precise timestamps from atomic clocks. By comparing exactly when each wave arrived at each telescope, a computer could combine them as if they had all been collected by one giant dish. The technique is called very long baseline interferometry.

> i: radio telescope | v: radio telescope night
The data were too large to send over the internet. About five petabytes, millions of gigabytes, stored on hard drives. They were flown to supercomputers in Massachusetts and Germany. [pause] The drives from the South Pole had to wait months, until the Antarctic winter ended and planes could fly in again to collect them.

> i: radio telescope array | v: radio telescope
The combined telescope had a resolution so fine that, from a café in Paris, it could in principle read a newspaper in New York. [pause] Turning that raw data into an image would take two more years.

## Four Teams, One Ring
mood: mystery
> i: computer data | v: data visualization
The trouble was that the network only had a handful of telescopes, spread thinly across the globe. Most of the giant virtual dish was missing. Reconstructing an image from such sparse data meant filling in the gaps with careful algorithms, without accidentally inventing what scientists expected to see.

> i: event horizon telescope | v: radio telescope
So the collaboration, around two hundred scientists, split into four independent imaging teams. Each was kept isolated from the others and worked on the data alone, with different methods. Only when they were finished did they compare results.

> i: m87 black hole | v: black hole
All four teams found the same thing. {{A ring|A RING}}. About forty microarcseconds across, with a dark centre. Brighter on one side, because the gas there was swirling towards us at nearly the speed of light. [pause] Exactly the shape general relativity predicted.

> i: m87 black hole | v: black hole accretion disk
One of the researchers, Katie Bouman, became a symbol of the project after a photograph of her watching the image appear went viral. She was quick to point out that it was a team effort, the work of many people over many years. [pause] Black holes are not photographed by one person. They are photographed by a planet.

## What The Picture Shows
mood: wonder
> i: black hole shadow | v: black hole
The dark centre is not the black hole itself. It is the black hole's shadow, a region where light that comes too close is captured or bent away from us. Its size depends on the black hole's mass.

> i: m87 black hole | v: black hole
By measuring it, the team weighed M87's black hole at about {{6.5 billion Suns|6.5 BILLION SUNS}}. Its event horizon is wider than our whole planetary system. The glowing ring is gas, heated to billions of degrees, orbiting just outside the edge of no return.

> i: hawaii telescope | v: night sky
The black hole was given a Hawaiian name, {{Powehi|PŌWEHI}}, meaning roughly the adorned, fathomless dark source of unending creation. It was chosen by a professor of the Hawaiian language, in honour of the telescopes on Hawaii that helped capture it.

> i: m87 black hole polarized | v: magnetic field
In 2021, the team released a new version, showing the direction of the light's polarisation. It revealed swirling magnetic fields threading the ring, strong enough to push back against the infalling gas. [pause] They may be part of how the black hole launches its colossal jet.

## Our Own Black Hole
mood: wonder
> i: sagittarius a black hole image | v: event horizon telescope
In May 2022, the Event Horizon Telescope revealed a second image. This time, the target was {{Sagittarius A*|SAGITTARIUS A*}}, the black hole at the centre of our own Milky Way, twenty six thousand light-years away.

> i: milky way center | v: milky way center
It was harder to photograph. Our black hole is more than a thousand times lighter than M87's, so the gas around it orbits in minutes rather than days, changing constantly while the telescopes watched. Astronomers compared it to trying to photograph a running puppy. But they managed it. Another glowing ring around a dark shadow.

> i: m87 black hole | v: black hole
Images of M87 from the following year showed the ring was still there, the same size, as predicted, but with its brightest part shifted around. The black hole was real, stable, and alive with motion. [pause] Astronomers are now working towards something even more ambitious. Not just photographs. Movies.

> i: event horizon telescope | v: radio telescope night
With more telescopes, and perhaps one day dishes in space, they hope to watch gas swirl around a black hole in real time, and see a jet being born. [pause] In 2019, we saw the shadow of a black hole for the first time. Soon, we may watch one breathe.
