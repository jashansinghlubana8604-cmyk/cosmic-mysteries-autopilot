## The Planet That Lost Its Water
mood: wonder
> v: mars ancient ocean | i: mars water
Today, Mars is a frozen desert. Its air is about one percent as thick as Earth's. Liquid water cannot last on its surface, because it would either freeze or boil away almost immediately.

> i: mars river valleys | v: mars valleys
But the surface tells a different story. Orbiting spacecraft have photographed dried riverbeds, branching valleys, deltas where rivers once flowed into lakes, and minerals that only form in water. Some scientists believe the northern half of Mars may once have held an ocean.

> v: mars ancient lake | i: jezero crater
Billions of years ago, Mars was warmer and wetter, with a thicker atmosphere. Then, something changed. Mars is smaller than Earth, and its interior cooled faster. Its global magnetic field faded, and without that shield, the solar wind slowly stripped its atmosphere away into space.

> i: mars atmosphere loss | v: solar wind mars
As the air thinned, the planet cooled and its water froze into the ground and the polar caps, or escaped into space. A world that may once have been habitable became the cold red desert we see today. [pause] The question is whether anything lived there while it could.

> v: mars dust storm | i: mars dust storm
Mars is still a violent place in its own way. Every few years, dust storms grow until they swallow the entire planet, blocking out the Sun for weeks. In 2018, one of these global storms buried the solar panels of the Opportunity rover, which had been exploring for almost fifteen years. Engineers sent more than a thousand commands, hoping it would wake up. [pause] It never did.

## The Gas That Comes And Goes
mood: mystery
> v: curiosity rover | i: curiosity rover
In Gale Crater, NASA's Curiosity rover has spent more than a decade sniffing the Martian air. And it found something puzzling: {{methane|METHANE}}.

> i: mars methane | v: mars atmosphere
On Earth, most methane is made by living things, from microbes to cows. It can also be made by geology, through reactions between water and certain rocks. On Mars, methane should be destroyed by sunlight within a few hundred years. So if it is there at all, something must be making it.

> v: gale crater | i: gale crater
Curiosity found faint traces that rose and fell with the seasons, and occasional sudden spikes. But a European orbiter, the Trace Gas Orbiter, designed specifically to detect methane from above, found almost none at all. The two results seem to contradict each other.

> i: mars surface | v: mars surface night
One possibility is that methane seeps from the ground at night, near the surface, and is destroyed or dispersed during the day before it can rise high enough for orbiters to see. Nobody knows for sure. Methane on Mars remains one of the most debated mysteries in planetary science.

> i: martian meteorite | v: meteorite
This would not be the first time Mars seemed to whisper about life. In 1996, scientists announced that a meteorite from Mars, found in Antarctica and known as ALH84001, contained tiny structures that looked like fossilised bacteria. The President of the United States spoke about it on television. Over the following years, most scientists concluded that the structures could have formed without life. The excitement faded. [pause] But the search did not.

## The Leopard Spots
mood: mystery
> v: perseverance rover | i: perseverance rover
In 2021, NASA's Perseverance rover landed in Jezero Crater, the site of an ancient lake, specifically to search for signs of past life.

> i: jezero crater delta | v: jezero crater
Jezero once held a lake fed by a river, which built a delta of layered sediment. On Earth, lakes and deltas like this are excellent at preserving traces of microbial life.

> v: mars rock | i: mars rock sample
In July 2024, Perseverance drilled into an arrowhead shaped rock nicknamed {{Cheyava Falls|CHEYAVA FALLS}}. On its surface were veins of calcium sulphate, left by water flowing through the rock. And scattered across it were small pale spots, each ringed with a dark border.

> i: leopard spots rock | v: mars rock close up
Scientists called them leopard spots. On Earth, markings like these can form when microbes use chemical reactions inside rock for energy. The rock also contained organic molecules, the carbon-based building blocks of life.

> v: perseverance sample | i: mars sample tube
In 2025, NASA announced that this was the clearest potential sign of ancient life yet found on Mars, while emphasising that non-living chemistry might still explain it. [pause] To be sure, scientists need to examine the sample in laboratories on Earth.

> i: mars surface | v: mars surface
If life did exist on Mars, it was almost certainly microscopic, like the earliest life on Earth. No forests, no animals, just single cells in ancient lakes and hot springs, billions of years ago. But even a single fossil microbe would change our understanding of life in the universe forever.

## Bringing It Home
mood: wonder
> i: mars sample return | v: mars sample return
Perseverance has been sealing rock samples into small tubes and leaving some of them on the surface, waiting for a future mission to collect them. That plan, called Mars Sample Return, would be one of the most complex missions ever attempted.

> v: mars rover tracks | i: mars rover tracks
But it has been repeatedly delayed, and its future is uncertain. For now, the best evidence for ancient Martian life sits in sealed tubes, on the surface of another planet, waiting.

> i: mars helicopter | v: ingenuity helicopter
Meanwhile, Mars continues to amaze. Perseverance carried a small helicopter, Ingenuity, which made the first powered flight on another planet in 2021. It was only meant to fly five times. It flew seventy two.

> v: olympus mons | i: olympus mons
And Mars still holds wonders that have nothing to do with life. Its volcano Olympus Mons is the tallest known mountain in the solar system, almost three times the height of Everest, so wide that standing on its slopes, you would not be able to see that you were on a mountain at all. Its canyon, Valles Marineris, would stretch across the entire United States.

## A Second Genesis?
mood: wonder
> v: mars | i: mars globe
Why does this matter so much? Because if life ever began on Mars, independently of Earth, it would mean life starts easily wherever conditions allow. The universe, with its countless planets, could be full of it.

> i: earth from space | v: earth rotation from space
Or it might reveal something stranger. Rocks have travelled between Mars and Earth, blasted off by impacts. Some scientists have wondered whether life could have hitched a ride, and whether our own ancestors might once have come from Mars. It is speculation. But it is not impossible.

> v: mars sunset | i: mars sunset
On Mars, sunsets are blue. The fine red dust scatters light so that the sky glows blue around the setting Sun. It is a quiet, alien beauty on a world that was once, perhaps, alive.

> i: mars surface | v: mars surface flyover
Mars is not the dead planet it once seemed. [pause] Somewhere in its ancient rocks may be the answer to one of the oldest questions we have ever asked: are we alone?
