## Hidden Behind The Centre
mood: mystery
> i: milky way center | v: milky way center
In 1994, astronomers Rodrigo Ibata, Gerry Gilmore and Mike Irwin were studying stars in the bulge of the Milky Way, the crowded region around its centre, in the direction of the constellation Sagittarius. They noticed a group of stars moving together, at a speed and distance that did not match the bulge.

> i: sagittarius dwarf galaxy | v: galaxy
They realised they had found a separate galaxy, lying beyond the galactic centre, around seventy thousand light-years from the Earth. It became known as the {{Sagittarius Dwarf Galaxy|SAGITTARIUS DWARF}}. At the time, it was the closest known galaxy to us.

> i: star field | v: star field
It had been hidden in plain sight. Spread across a huge area of sky, behind the dense stars of our own galaxy's centre, it was simply too faint and too diffuse to stand out. [pause] A whole galaxy, camouflaged by ours.

> i: telescope dome | v: telescope night
What gave it away was motion. By measuring how fast the stars were moving towards or away from us, the team found a large group all sharing the same speed, different from the stars of the bulge around them. [pause] Stars that travel together usually belong together. They were members of another galaxy.

## Being Torn Apart
mood: dread
> i: galaxy merger | v: galaxy merger
The Sagittarius Dwarf is on a looping orbit around the Milky Way, plunging through our galaxy's disc again and again over billions of years. Each time it passes, the Milky Way's gravity strips away more of its stars.

> i: sagittarius stream | v: milky way
Those stolen stars do not vanish. They spread out along its orbit, forming a gigantic ribbon called the {{Sagittarius Stream|SAGITTARIUS STREAM}}, which wraps around the entire Milky Way, more than once. It was mapped across the sky in the early 2000s, and it is one of the largest structures in our galactic neighbourhood.

> i: globular cluster | v: star cluster
At the heart of the dwarf lies a dense ball of stars called M54, a globular cluster, which may be the remains of the dwarf galaxy's core. Some studies have suggested it could even contain a black hole. [pause] The galaxy has already lost much of its mass. In time, the Milky Way will absorb it completely.

> i: sagittarius dwarf galaxy | v: galaxy
Before the Milky Way began tearing it apart, the Sagittarius Dwarf may have been far more massive, perhaps tens of billions of times the mass of the Sun once its dark matter is included. Much of that has already been stripped away. [pause] What is left is a shadow of what it once was.

> i: milky way | v: milky way galaxy
This is how big galaxies grow. Not just by making stars from gas, but by swallowing smaller galaxies. The Milky Way has eaten many over its history. [pause] The Sagittarius Dwarf is one of the few we can watch while it is still being digested.

## Ripples In The Disc
mood: wonder
> i: gaia spacecraft | v: gaia
The European Space Agency's {{Gaia|GAIA}} mission has measured the positions and motions of more than a billion stars in our galaxy. In 2018, astronomers using its data found something unexpected near the Sun: a subtle spiral pattern in how stars move up and down through the galactic disc.

> i: milky way galaxy | v: milky way galaxy
It looked like ripples spreading across a pond after a stone was dropped in. The disc of the Milky Way had been disturbed, a few hundred million years ago. And the most likely cause was a passage of the Sagittarius Dwarf, punching through the disc and setting the stars wobbling.

> i: milky way | v: milky way time-lapse
Some astronomers have even suggested that repeated passages of the dwarf helped shape the spiral arms of the Milky Way itself. [pause] A small galaxy, leaving its mark on a giant.

> i: galaxy merger | v: galaxy merger
It is not the first galaxy to have shaped ours. Gaia has also revealed the remains of a much bigger collision, around ten billion years ago, when the young Milky Way swallowed a galaxy now called Gaia-Enceladus. Its stars still move through our galaxy on strange, stretched orbits. [pause] The Milky Way is built from the wreckage of the galaxies it has eaten.

## The Birth Of The Sun
mood: wonder
> i: star forming region | v: nebula
In 2020, a team led by astronomer Tomás Ruiz-Lara used Gaia to reconstruct the history of star formation in our part of the galaxy. They found that the Milky Way did not make stars at a steady rate. It went through bursts of intense star formation.

> i: sagittarius dwarf galaxy | v: galaxy collision
Those bursts seemed to line up with times when the Sagittarius Dwarf passed close to or through the Milky Way's disc, around six billion, two billion and one billion years ago. As it crashed through, it may have squeezed clouds of gas, triggering them to collapse into new stars.

> i: sun | v: sun
And here is the extraordinary part. One of those episodes happened around four and a half to five billion years ago, close to the time our Sun was born. It is possible that a passage of the Sagittarius Dwarf helped trigger the collapse of the cloud that became our solar system. [pause] That idea is not proven. But it means a tiny galaxy, crashing through ours, may be part of the story of how we came to exist.

## The Last Meal
mood: mystery
> i: milky way | v: milky way galaxy
Today, the Sagittarius Dwarf is close to the Milky Way's disc again, on the far side of the galaxy. It is heavily damaged, stretched and thinned out. Eventually, it will be completely dissolved, its stars mixed into the Milky Way's halo.

> i: large magellanic cloud | v: large magellanic cloud
It is not the only one. The Large and Small Magellanic Clouds, bigger dwarf galaxies orbiting us, are also falling inward. In a few billion years, they too may be swallowed. [pause] Our galaxy is a predator, and it is always hungry.

> i: sagittarius stream | v: milky way
The Sagittarius Stream is also a powerful tool. Because it wraps around the whole galaxy, its shape traces the pull of everything the Milky Way contains, including its invisible halo of dark matter. [pause] By mapping the stream, astronomers can weigh our galaxy and work out the shape of the darkness surrounding it.

> i: night sky | v: night sky
