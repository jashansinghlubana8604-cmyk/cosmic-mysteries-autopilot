## The Pizza Moon
mood: wonder
> i: io galileo | v: io
Io was discovered by Galileo Galilei in January 1610, one of four little points of light circling Jupiter. For more than three centuries, it was nothing more than that. A dot. Slightly bigger than our own Moon.

> i: voyager jupiter | v: voyager jupiter
Then, in March 1979, Voyager 1 flew past. Its cameras revealed a world that looked like nothing anyone had ever seen. Yellow, orange, red, white and black. Blotched and stained. Scientists joked that it looked like a pizza.

> i: io surface | v: io surface
Those colours came from sulphur, in different forms and temperatures, painted over the whole surface. And there were no craters at all. Every other rocky body in the solar system is scarred by billions of years of impacts. Io's surface was fresh. [pause] Something was paving it over, again and again.

> i: io plume | v: io volcano
A few days after the flyby, a navigation engineer named {{Linda Morabito|LINDA MORABITO}} was studying an image to pin down Voyager's position. At the edge of Io, she noticed a strange, faint umbrella of light rising from the surface. [pause] It was a volcanic plume, hundreds of kilometres high. The first active volcano ever seen beyond the Earth.

> i: io volcano | v: io
Remarkably, three scientists had predicted it just days earlier. In a paper published right before the flyby, they argued that Jupiter's tides should be heating Io's interior so much that it might be covered in volcanoes. [pause] Rarely has a prediction come true so quickly.

## Squeezed By A Giant
mood: dread
> i: jupiter io | v: jupiter io
Io orbits Jupiter at roughly the same distance as our Moon orbits the Earth. But Jupiter is more than three hundred times heavier than our planet. Its gravity grips Io with enormous force.

> i: jupiter moons | v: jupiter moons
If Io moved in a perfect circle, that grip would be steady, and nothing much would happen. But it does not. Its neighbours, Europa and Ganymede, keep tugging it. For every orbit Ganymede makes, Europa makes two, and Io makes {{four|1 : 2 : 4}}. That rhythm keeps Io's orbit slightly stretched, forever.

> i: io | v: io rotation
So as Io swings closer to Jupiter and then further away, it is squeezed and released, squeezed and released. The solid ground itself rises and falls, by perhaps as much as a hundred metres, every forty two hours. Imagine the ground under your house lifting higher than a thirty-storey building, and sinking back, twice every few days.

> i: io volcano | v: lava
All that flexing creates friction, and friction creates heat. A colossal amount of it. Io pours out more heat from its surface, for its size, than any other world in the solar system. [pause] It is being kneaded like dough until its insides melt.

## Lakes Of Fire
mood: dread
> i: loki patera | v: lava lake
Io's most famous volcano is not a mountain. It is a hole. {{Loki Patera|LOKI PATERA}} is a depression about two hundred kilometres across, filled with a lake of lava. It is the most powerful volcano in the solar system, and it changes its brightness in a strange, regular rhythm.

> i: lava lake | v: lava
Scientists think the lava's surface cools and hardens into a crust, which grows heavier until it cracks and sinks, exposing fresh glowing lava underneath. A slow wave of overturning sweeps across the whole lake. [pause] A lake of fire bigger than many countries, turning itself over every few hundred days.

> i: io volcano | v: io volcano
Some of Io's lava is believed to reach around fifteen hundred degrees, hotter than any lava erupting on Earth today. That kind of super-hot lava last flowed on our own planet billions of years ago, when the Earth was young and raw.

> i: io plume new horizons | v: io plume
And the plumes. In 2007, the New Horizons spacecraft, on its way to Pluto, caught a giant plume from a volcano called {{Tvashtar|TVASHTAR}}, rising more than three hundred kilometres above Io. With such weak gravity and no air to slow it down, the material simply arcs high into space and rains back down over an area the size of a country.

## The Closest Look
mood: mystery
> i: juno spacecraft | v: juno spacecraft
For decades, Io was too dangerous to visit up close. It sits deep inside Jupiter's radiation belts, a storm of charged particles strong enough to fry electronics and kill an unprotected human within days.

> i: juno io | v: juno jupiter
But at the end of 2023 and the start of 2024, NASA's Juno spacecraft dived past Io twice, coming within about one thousand five hundred kilometres of its surface. The images showed mountains, jagged peaks and fresh lava flows in astonishing detail.

> i: io surface juno | v: io
They also captured Loki Patera's lake looking almost like a mirror. Scientists think the cooled crust on parts of the lake may be smooth and glassy, like volcanic obsidian. [pause] A black glass sea, reflecting the light of Jupiter.

> i: io interior | v: io interior
Juno also tested a big idea. For years, many scientists believed Io had a global ocean of magma just beneath its crust. By measuring Io's gravity as it flew past, Juno found that this is unlikely. Instead, the inside seems mostly solid, with the magma feeding each volcano coming from its own chamber. [pause] The textbook picture had been wrong.

> i: io volcano | v: io volcano
And at the end of 2024, Juno's infrared camera spotted an enormous new hot region in Io's southern hemisphere, glowing with energy measured in tens of trillions of watts. It may be among the most powerful volcanic events ever recorded on that moon.

## The Electric Moon
mood: wonder
> i: io torus | v: jupiter magnetosphere
Io does something else no other moon does. Its volcanoes throw out about a tonne of material every second. Much of it gets stripped away by Jupiter's magnetic field and spread into a giant glowing ring that follows Io's orbit all the way around the planet.

> i: jupiter aurora | v: jupiter aurora
And as Io moves through Jupiter's magnetic field, it acts like a giant electrical generator. A current of millions of amps flows along the magnetic field lines between Io and Jupiter. Where it hits Jupiter's atmosphere, it lights up a glowing spot in the planet's aurora. [pause] {{Io's footprint|IO'S FOOTPRINT}}, burned into Jupiter's sky.

> i: io jupiter moon | v: io
So here is a moon slightly bigger than our own, being squeezed by a giant, melting from the inside, painting itself in sulphur, and wiring itself into its planet's sky with a current of millions of amps. [pause] It is not a dead rock. It is a world in a permanent state of eruption.

> i: io volcano | v: jupiter io
And it will stay that way for as long as Jupiter, Europa and Ganymede keep up their rhythm. Every forty two hours, the squeeze. Every day, new lava. [pause] Somewhere on Io, right now, a new volcano is being born.
