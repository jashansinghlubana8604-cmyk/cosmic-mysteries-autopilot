## A Giant Too Close
mood: wonder
> i: exoplanet transit | v: exoplanet transit
{{WASP-76b|WASP-76b}} was discovered in 2013 by the Wide Angle Search for Planets, a project using small robotic telescopes to watch stars for tiny dips in brightness, the shadows of passing planets.

> i: hot jupiter | v: hot jupiter
It is a gas giant, slightly less massive than Jupiter, but nearly twice as wide. The intense heat from its star has puffed it up like a balloon. It orbits its star at only about one twelfth of Mercury's distance from the Sun.

> i: hot jupiter | v: hot jupiter
Planets like this were a shock to astronomers. In 1995, the first planet ever found orbiting a Sun-like star, called 51 Pegasi b, turned out to be a giant planet circling its star in just four days. Nobody had predicted such worlds. The discovery won Michel Mayor and Didier Queloz the Nobel Prize in Physics in 2019. [pause] Since then, hundreds of these so-called hot Jupiters have been found.

> i: solar system formation | v: solar system formation
Giant planets almost certainly cannot form that close to a star. It is too hot for the ice and gas they are made from to gather. So scientists think planets like WASP-76b were born much further out, then migrated inward, perhaps dragged by the disc of gas around the young star, or thrown inward by encounters with other planets. [pause] They are giants that ended up in the wrong place.

> i: star | v: star
Its star, called WASP-76, is a bit bigger and hotter than our Sun. From the planet's day side, it would fill an enormous part of the sky, blazing down without ever setting, because the planet is {{tidally locked|TIDALLY LOCKED}}. One half in endless day. The other in endless night.

> i: exoplanet atmosphere | v: exoplanet atmosphere
The temperature difference between the two sides drives some of the most violent winds imaginable, blowing at thousands of kilometres per hour from the scorching day side towards the night. [pause] And those winds carry something astonishing.

## Iron Rain
mood: dread
> i: telescope dome | v: telescope night
In 2020, a team led by astronomer David Ehrenreich used a new instrument called {{ESPRESSO|ESPRESSO}}, on the Very Large Telescope in Chile, to study the light filtering through WASP-76b's atmosphere as the planet passed in front of its star.

> i: exoplanet atmosphere | v: exoplanet atmosphere
They detected iron vapour. But there was a strange asymmetry. In the morning, along the boundary where the night side rotates into the day, there was much less iron vapour than in the evening, where the day side rotates into the night.

> i: wasp-76b | v: hot jupiter
The best explanation was dramatic. On the day side, iron evaporates. Winds carry the vapour across to the night side, which is cooler, around fifteen hundred degrees. There, the iron condenses into liquid droplets and rains out of the sky. By the time the air comes back around into the morning, much of its iron is gone. [pause] It has fallen as rain.

> i: lava | v: lava
Imagine standing on the night side, if you could stand anywhere on a gas giant. Sheets of glowing metal droplets falling through the dark, lit faintly red by their own heat. [pause] Not water. Not methane. Molten iron, falling from the sky.

> i: wasp-76b | v: exoplanet orbit
And everything there happens fast. The planet completes a full orbit around its star in about one point eight Earth days. A birthday every forty three hours, on a world where the sun never moves in the sky.

## A Periodic Table In The Sky
mood: wonder
> i: exoplanet atmosphere | v: exoplanet atmosphere
WASP-76b turned out to be a treasure trove for chemists. Because it is so hot, many metals that would be solid on cooler planets are floating in its atmosphere as gases, where telescopes can detect them.

> i: exoplanet atmosphere | v: exoplanet atmosphere
Over the following years, astronomers identified more than a dozen elements in its air. Sodium, calcium, magnesium, chromium, nickel, manganese, vanadium, even barium, one of the heaviest elements ever detected in an exoplanet atmosphere. [pause] Its sky is like a page from the periodic table.

> i: wasp-76b | v: hot jupiter
And one element was conspicuously missing: titanium. Scientists think titanium may condense into minerals on the cooler night side and sink deep into the planet, getting trapped below the visible atmosphere. [pause] If iron rains, titanium may rain too, and never come back up.

## The Glory
mood: mystery
> i: glory rainbow | v: clouds
In 2024, astronomers using the European Space Agency's CHEOPS space telescope reported something even more remarkable. Watching how the planet's brightness changed as it orbited, they found a hint of an extra glow on its eastern edge. CHEOPS is a small space telescope dedicated to measuring the brightness of nearby stars with planets, with extreme precision, again and again, over many orbits.

> i: glory rainbow | v: clouds
They suggested it might be a {{glory|A GLORY}}. On Earth, a glory is a ring of rainbow colours you can sometimes see around the shadow of an aeroplane on clouds below, or around your own shadow on mist from a mountaintop. It forms only when light bounces off perfectly round, uniform droplets.

> i: wasp-76b | v: exoplanet atmosphere
If confirmed, it would be the first glory ever seen on a planet outside our solar system. And it would mean that somewhere in WASP-76b's atmosphere, there are clouds of perfectly spherical droplets, perhaps of iron. [pause] A rainbow made by metal rain.

## Why It Matters
mood: wonder
> i: hot jupiter | v: hot jupiter
Planets like WASP-76b, called ultra-hot Jupiters, have nothing in common with anywhere life could exist. So why study them? Because they are natural laboratories for physics and chemistry at extremes we can never reproduce on Earth.

> i: webb telescope | v: webb telescope
They are also the easiest exoplanet atmospheres to study. They are large, very hot and bright, and they pass in front of their stars often. The techniques astronomers perfect on worlds like this one are the same techniques they will use to study smaller, cooler, potentially habitable planets.

> i: exoplanet artist impression | v: exoplanet
And they remind us how strange the universe is. When the first planets around other stars were found, nobody imagined worlds with iron rain, metal skies and rainbows of molten droplets. [pause] Every new world teaches us that our solar system is just one way that planets can be. Out there, the weather is stranger than any fiction.

> i: red giant star | v: red giant
And its story will not end quietly. Its star is ageing, and over hundreds of millions of years, it will slowly grow larger and brighter. One day, it may swell enough to swallow the planet completely. [pause] A world that rains iron, eventually consumed by the sun it circles.
