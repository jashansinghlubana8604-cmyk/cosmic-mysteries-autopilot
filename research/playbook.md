# The proven formula (from the space-mystery outliers)

Built from `research/report.md` (1,167 videos ranked by views ÷ subscribers) and the full transcripts of the top
outliers: Saturn "Scariest Planet" (6.2M, 62×), "NASA Put Spiders in Zero Gravity" (2.1M, 716×), "Something Weird
Is Happening On Europa" (3.1M), "NASA Just Confirmed Voyager 1 Found Something Impossible" (4.5M), "The Scariest
Places in the Universe" (3.7M, 155×), "Every Terrifying Type of Black Hole" (1.1M, 71×), "James Webb Spent 100 Hours
Staring at Empty Space" (390K, 390×). The writer (`cosmos/writer.py`) reads this file; keep it current.

## 1. Topics that win (in order of proven pull)

1. **Voyager** (four separate 1.6–4.5M outliers): edge of the solar system, a strange signal, its far future.
2. **"Scariest / most terrifying"** lists and places: planets, sounds, places, black holes.
3. **A familiar world reframed as terrifying or strange**: Saturn, Venus, Europa, Mars, Titan, Pluto.
4. **Biggest / rarest / most extreme**: TON 618, biggest stars, rarest things, size comparisons.
5. **James Webb found something that "shouldn't exist"**: galaxies, black-hole stars, "empty" space.
6. **Signals and objects we can't explain**: Wow!, FRBs, 'Oumuamua, 3I/ATLAS, Tabby's star.
7. **The end / edge / outside of the universe**: vacuum decay, heat death, what's beyond.
8. **Recent discoveries (last 30 days)** in the same frames. News beats evergreen when it is fresh.

Avoid: abstract physics with no single object to show, topics with no real footage, people-only stories.

## 2. Titles (CTR)

Patterns that repeat across the winners. Use one, under 60 characters, and make it true:

- `Something Strange Is Happening on/at X` / `Something Weird Is Happening on X`
- `X Found Something No One Expected` / `NASA Just Confirmed X Found Something Impossible`
- `Why X Is the Scariest Planet` / `The Most Terrifying Places in the Universe`
- `James Webb Found X That Shouldn't Exist` / `James Webb Just Spent 100 Hours Staring at 'Empty' Space`
- `X: The Black Hole That Breaks the Universe`
- `Every Type of X Explained` (list videos)
- `What Is Outside the Universe?` (big plain question)

Rules: a concrete noun people know (Voyager, Saturn, James Webb, black hole) in the first three words; one
emotional word (strange, terrifying, impossible, shouldn't exist); never fake news ("NASA in PANIC", "BREAKING"),
never a claim the video does not back up.

## 3. Thumbnails (CTR)

- **One striking subject**, huge and centred or on one side. Real imagery beats drawings in this niche.
- **2–4 words max**, never repeating the title. White, very heavy, with ONE accent word in yellow/gold.
  The words add the emotion the image lacks: `TOO BIG TO EXIST`, `THE SKY ANSWERED`, `NOT EMPTY`.
- Proven layouts: (a) cinematic: wide-spaced words on the lower third (NullSpace "LONG AFTER US / VOYAGER 1",
  2M views); (b) headline: big heavy words on top, subject below ("THIS IS EUROPA", 3.1M); (c) annotated: a red
  circle or arrow on the strange detail ("WEBB CAN'T LOOK ANY FURTHER", Voyager "found something"); (d) grid for
  "every type / scariest places" lists.
- High contrast, saturated, glowing subject on black. It must read at phone size (168×94).
- No fake news-channel logos, no "LIVE" badges, nothing that misrepresents the content.

## 4. The first 45 seconds (retention)

Every winner opens with one of these, then states the stakes, then opens a loop it will close later:

- **Right now** + present tense + "you": "Right now, while you're watching this, a machine built before the
  internet is crossing a region no model predicted…"
- **A sound or image and a reframe**: "Listen to that. That is not a soundtrack. That is Saturn… We called it
  beautiful. But beauty is the ultimate trap."
- **Cold story with a date and a person**: "August 1973. Inside Skylab, astronaut Owen Garriott opened a vial…"
- **The impossible fact**: "It weighs sixty-six billion Suns. It shouldn't exist."

Then within 20 seconds: why it matters / what is at stake, and a promise ("and what they found next changed
everything we thought we knew about…"). The title's promise must be visibly on its way inside the first minute.

## 5. The body (average view duration)

- **Escalate.** Each chapter is stranger, bigger or scarier than the last. Save the best reveal for chapter 4–5.
- **Twist beats every 60–90 s**: "That should have been the end of the story. It wasn't." / "But the strangeness
  goes deeper." / "And that's when the data stopped making sense."
- **"You" inside the scene**: "If you stood on Titan…", "If you jumped into that lake, you would shatter like glass."
- **Vivid, physical metaphors**: rings = "a grinder", "the scattered guts of a dead moon", "a rose made of
  hurricanes", "less power than the light in your fridge". One per paragraph at most.
- **Numbers made human**: always pair a big number with something the viewer has touched.
- **Short sentences. Strong verbs.** Mix 4-word punches with 20-word flowing lines. Read it aloud.
- **Honesty is the brand**: hedge uncertain claims ("one study suggested", "astronomers estimate"); the twist is
  the real science, never invented drama.
- **End on awe, not a summary**: a final image or thought that lingers ("look at it while you can").

## 6. Length, pace and edit

- 10–13 minutes (≥ 8 minutes for mid-roll ads). 1,350–1,750 spoken words.
- Narration pace: atmospheric winners run 105–150 wpm with music; energetic ones 160–195 wpm. Ours: natural voice
  speed with short breaths (~140–150 wpm overall).
- Picture changes every 4–6 s in the open, 5–7 s in the body; real footage and moving shots first.
- On-screen words only for numbers, names and one-word reveals (2–4 per chapter).
- Title sequence and chapter cards short (≤ 7 s and ≤ 3.5 s): every second without new information loses viewers.

## 7. Sound

- A dark drone + riser under the cold open, a sub boom on every reveal, whooshes on cuts between sections.
- Real space audio where it exists (Voyager plasma waves, Saturn radio emission, Cassini, pulsars) is the best hook
  of all; describe it in the script ("Listen to this…") when the subject has it.
- Music by mood, ducked under the voice, rising in the gaps; mastered to −14 LUFS.

## 8. Posting

- One video a day at the same window (04:15–05:00 IST = evening in the US, the biggest audience for English space
  content).
- The first 48 hours decide distribution: the title + thumbnail must match what the first 30 seconds deliver.
